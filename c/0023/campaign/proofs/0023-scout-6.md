## A. VERDICT

**On (Q): SETTLED — (Q) is KNOWN. It is a theorem, printed as an explicit
corollary by O'Donnell–Saks–Schramm–Servedio in 2005**, with
`p(D) = O(D^4)` in their own sentence and `p(D) = O(D^3)` using the current
best degree-vs-decision-tree-depth bound. Not open, not false.

**On the classification the errand asked for: (Q) is the Boolean-valued
(±1-valued), variance-1 special case of the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, and
is STRICTLY WEAKER than AA.** AA ⇒ (Q) by an affine change of value range
(one line, in card S8c); (Q) does not imply AA, and AA has remained open the
whole time (Q) has been known. The campaign's earlier marker — `0023-scout-1`
§B4 and Contract K4, "AA open since 2009" — stands for AA but **must not be
transferred to (Q)**: AA's open regime is **bounded real-valued** functions
with a variance factor; the ±1-valued case is settled.

**On R2 / (PAY★★): NO PRIOR RESOLUTION FOUND — unchanged.** This sweep did not

## B. Nearest known results, each with the exact gap

**B1. THE HIT. OSSS, arXiv:cs/0508071v1, §1.2.1, p. 4 [READ — all 11 pages as
page images].** Printed verbatim: "*Since `D(f) <= O(deg(f)^4)` (by a result of
Nisan and Smolensky [8]), our Corollary 1.2 implies that the maximum influence
in fact satisfies `Inf_max(f) >= Omega(Var[f]/deg(f)^4)`.*" With their printed
conventions (p. 2) `Var[f] = 4Pr[f=1]Pr[f=-1] = 1` for balanced ±1-valued `f`,
and `Inf_i(f) = Pr[f(x) != f(x⊕i)] = sum_{S ∋ i} fhat(S)^2` at `p = 1/2` —
i.e. **exactly the Contract's Fourier influence** on ±1-valued functions.
**Gap to (Q): none.** The engine is Corollary 1.2 (p. 3),
`Delta(f) >= Var(f)/Inf_max(f)`, from Theorem 1.1 (p. 3),
`Var[f] <= sum_i delta_i(T) Inf_i(f)` for **any** DDT `T` computing `f` — no
monotonicity, no balance, no degree hypothesis, `n`-free. Card **S9**.

**B2. Best printed exponent: `Inf_max >= Omega(Var/D^3)`.** Chugh–Podder–
Sanyal, arXiv:2209.08042v1, p. 2 [READ]: "*for all Boolean functions `f`,
`D(f) = O(bs(f)^3)` and `D(f) = O(deg(f)^3)`. It is also known that there exist
Boolean functions `g,h` such that `D(g) = Omega(bs(g)^2)` and
`D(h) = Omega(deg(h)^2)`.*" **Gap:** the `O(deg^3)` bound is stated as known
there, citing Table 1 of Aaronson–Ben-David–Kothari–Rao–Tal, STOC 2021
[RESTATED, table not read]; the common attribution to **Midrijānis 2004 is
[MEMORY] only and I could not confirm it** — I fetched
`arXiv:quant-ph/0403168`, whose abstract states a quantum-to-classical
`O(T^3)` simulation, *not* the degree bound. Consequence recorded in S8b:
**cite Chugh–Podder–Sanyal p. 2 or OSSS's own `deg^4`, never Midrijānis.**
The `Omega(deg^2)` separation caps this route at exponent 2.

**B3. Stronger than (Q), same page [READ].** OSSS p. 4 also prints
`Inf_max(f) >= Omega(Var[f]/degtilde(f)^6)` for the **approximate** degree of a
±1-valued `f` (via `D(f) <= O(degtilde(f)^6)`, Beals et al.). **Gap:** relevant
if a future rung replaces "degree `<= d`" by "1/3-approximated in degree `d`";
it does *not* extend to real-valued functions.

**B4. The AA conjecture, exact variants, still open [READ x3].**
Bhattacharya, ITCS 2025 (LIPIcs 325:17), p. 17:3, Conjecture 4: "*Let
`f : {±1}^n -> [0,1]` be a degree `d` polynomial. Then, there exists a
coordinate `j` such that `Inf_j[f] >= poly(1/d, Var[f])`*"; p. 17:2,
Conjecture 3 is the depth-`poly(d,1/eps)` decision-tree approximation form; and
p. 17:3 prints the equivalence: "*O'Donnell et al. [18] had shown previously
that functions which can be approximated by decision trees have a coordinate
with high influence. So conjectures 3 and 4 are equivalent.*" Lovett–Zhang,
ECCC TR22-107, p. 2 [READ]: "*The AA conjecture is known to be true for
Boolean functions … However, their proof technique does not extend to bounded
functions.*" Aaronson's blog `?p=4414` [READ, rendered text]: AA is for
`p(x) ∈ [0,1]`, and records the Keller–Klein retraction (fatal flaw in
Lemma 5.3, found by Ivanishvili). **Gap to (Q):** the value range and the
variance factor — precisely the two things (Q) does not have.

**B5. Why the real-valued case is not a corollary of B1 [READ].** OSSS §3.4 and
**Figure 1, p. 10**: for real-valued `f` in the variance semimetric
`rho_2(z,z') = (z-z')^2/2` the inequality **fails** — an explicit
`f : {-1,1}^3 -> R` computed by a displayed DDT has `Var[f] = 3/2` but
`sum_i delta_i(T) Inf_i^{rho_2}(f) = 23/16 < 3/2`. The repaired real-valued
form (Corollary 3.4, p. 9) pays a factor `k` (longest DDT path), giving only
`Var[f]/k^2`, and **requires an exact decision tree for `f`**, which a bounded
degree-`d` real function need not admit at any small depth. **This is the
precise mathematical reason (Q) is a theorem while AA is open** — the most
useful single fact in this sweep after B1.

**B6. The junta + Poincaré fallback the errand predicted: correct, and
exponentially weaker.** Balanced ±1-valued `g` has `sum_i Inf_i(g) >= Var = 1`
and at most `4.394 * 2^D` relevant coordinates (card **S6c** [READ], Wellens,
Discrete Analysis 2022:19, Thm 1.1), so `Inf_max(g) >= 2^{-D-2.14}`. **The
errand's expected reasoning is confirmed; the gap to B1/B2 is a full
exponential.** Recorded as S8d.

**B7. Upper bound on how good (Q) can be (SCOUT'S OWN COMPUTATION, flagged,
not a citation).** `k`-fold read-once composition of `MAJ_3` is balanced with

exponent lies in `[0.631, 3]`; the exponent is **not** pinned in print.

**B8. Nothing found for the two-family statement.** No paper printing R2,

## C. Standard techniques on this family, and what obstructs each

1. **OSSS + degree-vs-depth (the winner for (Q)).** Obstruction where it
   stops: the Boolean-valued hypothesis (S9 hypothesis 1) and the existence of
   an exact decision tree (hypothesis 2). Neither survives to real-valued
   functions (B5) or to R3's level-set functions.
2. **Nisan–Szegedy junta counting + Poincaré.** Structurally capped at
   `2^{-Theta(D)}`: the per-coordinate influence quantum is `2^{1-D}` and is
   attained, and there really are `Theta(2^D)` relevant coordinates (cards
   S6a/S6c/S6d). Cannot reach `1/poly` — B6.
3. **Hypercontractivity / KKL / Talagrand.** Gives `Inf_max >=
   exp(-O(Inf/Var))`, i.e. only `exp(-O(D))` here; OSSS p. 4 makes exactly
   this comparison and beats it. Obstruction: these bounds are blind to the
   computational structure of `f`.
4. **Random restrictions (DFKO06 / Bhattacharya).** Yields `exp(d)`-type or
   for-most-restrictions statements, not for-all — the same reason it does not
   settle AA. Already recorded in `0023-scout-1` §B4.
5. **Block-multilinear / completely bounded routes (Montanaro; Bansal–Sinha–de
   Wolf).** Restrict the polynomial's shape; irrelevant to (Q), which is now
   a theorem, and unhelpful for R2's indicators.
