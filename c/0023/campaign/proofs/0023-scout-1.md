## A. VERDICT: NO PRIOR RESOLUTION FOUND

The PCC (ACC22 Conjectures 1.2/4.3/5.5 = CLM23 Conjecture 2.8) is neither proved
nor refuted in the inverse-polynomial influence regime anywhere I could find,
2022 through 2026-08-27. The proved positive regime remains exponential
($\delta < |\mathcal{Y}|^{-d}/d$, ACC22 Theorem 4.4 [READ]); the refutation
frontier remains $\delta \ge 1/(2d)$ for $\mathbb{Z}_2$ (ACC22 Claim B.3 [READ]).
Strong corroboration that it is STILL open: three separate 2026 papers whose
whole point is to REMOVE the conjecture from its downstream applications rather
than prove it — arXiv:2504.05710 v2 (June 2026) "Our result removes the
conjecture used in [...]" [RESTATED, extraction of arxiv.org/html/2504.05710v2];
arXiv:2608.03824 (Aug 2026) "improving the two-round result in [LLLL26] and
removing the conjecture" [RESTATED, extraction of arxiv.org/html/2608.03824v1];
arXiv:2608.17610 (Aug 2026) bypasses via restricted-query settings [RESTATED,
extraction of arxiv.org/html/2608.17610v1]. Nobody removes a conjecture that has
been proved. The repo's 2026-08-19 literature check is RE-VERIFIED and extended:
two papers newer than that check (2608.17610 of Aug 18, 2608.21241 of Aug 21)
also do not touch the conjecture itself.

Statement-provenance finding (Contract obligation, not a STOP): the Contract's
verbatim statement is CLM23's Conjecture 2.8 ($\mathbb{C}$-valued functions),
not ACC22's printed Conjecture 5.5 ($\mathbb{R}$-valued). The two are equivalent
up to a constant factor in $\delta$ (ACC22 Theorem 5.6 + Appendix A; CLM23
footnote 8) [READ, both]. "Finitely supported" is a Contract-added convention:
neither paper prints it; in every printed application the distributions arise
from measuring a finite register and are finitely supported. Benign, but
downstream write-ups should cite the statement as "CLM23 Conj. 2.8 form of
ACC22 Conj. 5.5".

## B. Nearest known results, with exact gaps

B1. **ACC22 Theorem 4.4** [READ, eprint.iacr.org/2022/218.pdf p. 20]: for ALL
finite Abelian groups $\mathcal{Y}$, all $d, N$, and $\delta <
|\mathcal{Y}|^{-d}/d$ (strict), any two $(\mathcal{Y}, \delta, d, N)$-states are
compatible; equivalently the polynomial statement at that threshold. Confirms
K1, with two corrections to the repo note: the inequality is strict, and it is
proved universally per-group (not "per-group wanted"). The proof (§5.2, p. 26)
uses LESS than the conjecture's hypotheses: only one $f \in \mathrm{supp}(F)$ of
degree $\le d$, no influence bound on $F$, no degree bound on $G$. GAP to the
Contract: threshold $|\mathcal{Y}|^{-d}/d$ vs required $1/\mathrm{poly}(d)$ —
the entire content of the conjecture.

B2. **ACC22 Claim B.3** [READ, p. 41]: for every $d$, the singleton pair
NegRow/PosCol on $d \times d$ Boolean variables has degree $d$, all relative
influences $\le 1/(2d)$, and $f \cdot g \equiv 0$. Confirms K2 with one material
caveat: PRINTED FOR $\mathbb{Z}_2$ ONLY. Because the group is existentially
quantified in the conjecture, the printed counterexample pins $\delta(d) <
1/(2d)$ (hence effectively $c_2 \ge 1$) only for the $\mathbb{Z}_2$
instantiation; no other group's ceiling is in print. Claims B.4/B.5 (p. 42)
[READ] show both the degree and the influence hypotheses are needed on BOTH
functions. GAP: the open window for $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is
$[|\,\mathbb{Z}_2|^{-d}/d,\ 1/(2d))$; for every other group the printed window
is even wider at the top.

B3. **CLM23 Lemma 2.12 + Conjecture 2.8** [READ, eprint.iacr.org/2023/570.pdf
pp. 14–16]: the $(\mathcal{Y}, \delta, d, N)$-state formulation and the
state-to-polynomial conversion; consumed at $\varepsilon = \delta(d\kappa)/10$,
i.e., strictly in the inverse-polynomial regime, group $\mathbb{Z}_2$ first.
Confirms K3. GAP: conversion adds no strength; one direction only.

B4. **Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture (the named kin) — still open at
inverse-polynomial influence.** Known cases: exponentially small influences
[RESTATED: DFKO06 via ACC22 p. 11, which I read]; completely bounded
block-multilinear forms with influence $\ge 1/\mathrm{poly}(d)$
[RESTATED: arXiv:2203.00212 (Bansal–Sinha–de Wolf) and arXiv:2304.06713
(Escudero Gutiérrez), via search results and abstracts]; random restrictions —
Bhattacharya, ITCS 2025, arXiv:2402.13952 [RESTATED via abstract + ECCC
TR24-035]: for degree-$d$ $f$ with sufficient variance, a random restriction
with survival probability $\log d / (C_1 d)$ has, with non-negligible
probability, a coordinate of influence $\ge \mathrm{Var}[f]^2 / d^{C_2}$.
Re-verifies the repo's K4. A Keller–Klein claimed proof of AA was RETRACTED
(fatal flaw found by Ivanishvili) [RESTATED via scottaaronson.blog/?p=4414
search snippet — cautionary datum, not load-bearing]. GAP: ACC22 itself prints
(p. 11 [READ]) that PCC and AA "do not seem to be directly comparable"; neither
implies the other in print, so no AA progress transfers automatically.

B5. **The 2026 bypass line (Li–Li–Li–Liu and successors).**
arXiv:2504.05710 v2 [RESTATED, extraction]: unconditional impossibility of
perfectly complete QPKE in the QROM via "Lemma 3.4 (win-win)": for any nonzero
degree-$d$ $f : \{0,1\}^N \to \mathbb{R}$ there is an explicit partial
assignment $\mu$, $|\mu| \le m d^2$, such that either $f(x^\mu) \ne 0$ for all
$x$, or every $x$ admits $m$ pairwise disjoint size-$\le d$ partial assignments
$\mu_\ell$ with $f(x^{\mu_\ell \cdot \mu}) \ne 0$. arXiv:2608.03824 [RESTATED,
extraction]: unconditional $O((q_A + q_B)^5)$-query classical attack on ANY
perfectly complete QCCC key agreement — the exact application ACC22 needed PCC
for — via transcript rectangularity + "Lemma 3.5", a decision-tree procedure
that, given query access, decides which of two disjoint-support degree-$d$
polynomials is nonzero in $O(d^4)$ queries. arXiv:2608.17610
(Kitagawa–Nishimaki–Villanyi–Yamakawa) [RESTATED, extraction]: unconditional
attacks for imperfect completeness in restricted-query settings; explicitly
frames the fully general imperfect case as blocked by the Simulation
Conjecture barrier (consistent with ACC22 Theorem 1.4). GAP, in both
directions: these are PER-FUNCTION, QUERY-ALGORITHMIC statements — they trade
the conjecture's "one common point for some pair from two distributions,
$\delta$ independent of $N$" for adaptive access to the oracle and to single
polynomials. None proves any influence regime of PCC; conversely PCC is now
NOT needed for perfectly complete QCCC key agreement, which weakens (but does
not eliminate: CLM23's commitments separation and ePrint 2023/1720's QPKE
separation still consume it) the conjecture's downstream load.

B6. **Negative results dating it open**: ePrint 2023/1720 (Bouaziz-Ermann,
Grilo, Vergnaud, Vu) works "under the polynomial compatibility conjecture"
[READ, ePrint abstract page], treating it as unproven in Nov 2023. ePrint
full-text search for "polynomial compatibility" returns only 2023/570 and
2023/1720 [READ, eprint.iacr.org/search] — no ePrint paper claims progress on
the conjecture itself.

## C. Standard techniques on this family, and what obstructs each

C1. *Maximal-monomial restriction + constant-block domination* (the ACC22 §5.2
proof [READ]). Fix a maximal character of $f$; show the restriction of $g$ to
the complementary coordinates has its constant Fourier block dominating all
others pointwise somewhere. Obstruction (visible in the displayed inequalities,
p. 26): the constant block must dominate all $|\mathcal{Y}|^d - 1$ nonconstant
blocks SIMULTANEOUSLY, and Cauchy–Schwarz across the blocks costs the factor
$|\mathcal{Y}|^d$, forcing $\delta \lesssim |\mathcal{Y}|^{-d}/d$. Claim B.3
shows this is not an artifact: at $\delta = 1/(2d)$ the conclusion itself fails
($\mathbb{Z}_2$), so any improvement must use the influence bound on BOTH
distributions and both degree bounds — i.e., must abandon the asymmetric
hypothesis structure that makes §5.2 work.

C2. *Uncertainty principles (Donoho–Stark)* — on record in CLM23 §2.3 [READ] as
an AMPLIFIER only: given $f_0 f_1 \not\equiv 0$ it lower-bounds the number of
common non-vanishing points, but is powerless to produce the first one, and its
naive strengthening ("low-degree supports always intersect") is FALSE by
NegRow/PosCol. No paper found that pushes an uncertainty-principle route past
this; no paper found recording a hypercontractivity route as failing (the
absence is itself the finding — the barrier file's "none formalized" stands).

C3. *AA-style influence lower bounds.* Proved at inverse-polynomial influence
only for completely bounded (block-)multilinear forms [RESTATED,
arXiv:2203.00212, 2304.06713] — the completely-bounded property comes from the
quantum-algorithm provenance, which PCC's state polynomials share; whether
state-polynomial distributions inherit enough of it is, as far as I can find,
unexplored in print. Random-restriction AA [RESTATED, arXiv:2402.13952] holds
only with non-negligible probability over restrictions — a for-some statement
where PCC needs for-all-distribution pairs.

C4. *Per-function algorithmic reprogramming* (2026 line, B5): win-win/decision
-tree lemmas prove strong structural facts about SINGLE low-degree polynomials
(explicit small partial assignments forcing non-vanishing). Obstruction to
lifting to PCC: the conjecture's difficulty is distributional simultaneity —
one $x$ must work for a PAIR drawn from supports of two adversarially
correlated-by-nothing distributions with only average influence control, with
$\delta$ independent of $N$; the 2026 lemmas instead adaptively choose
assignments per function. Possible transfer surface all the same (the only
genuinely new low-degree machinery on this exact family since 2022).

C5. *Group-hopping.* ACC22 Lemma 4.8 [READ, p. 21] transfers ATTACKS across
groups, not the conjecture. No printed reduction of PCC-for-$\mathcal{Y}$ to
PCC-for-$\mathbb{Z}_2$ or vice versa. Since the group is existentially
quantified, a prover may pick a large or structured group — unexplored in
print; note the counterexample ceiling $1/(2d)$ is only printed for
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ (B2), so the "effectively $c_2 \ge 1$" heuristic is not proven
for other groups.
