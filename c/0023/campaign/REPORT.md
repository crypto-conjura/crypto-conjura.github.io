# REPORT — c/0023: Polynomial Compatibility Conjecture (PCC)

**Date:** 2026-08-27 (cycles 1–3). **Status:** one rung of six established and frozen; a second rung partly proved and open; the root conjecture remains open.

## Goal

The Polynomial Compatibility Conjecture, in its inverse-polynomial-influence form, is a statement about pairs of random low-degree polynomials over a finite abelian group. Fix a finite abelian group and let functions on the group's N-fold product carry their natural notions of degree and of influence (how much of a function's Fourier energy depends on a given input coordinate). The conjecture says: there is a choice of group and a threshold function δ(d), decaying no faster than an inverse polynomial in the degree d, such that whenever two probability distributions **F** and **G** over unit-norm, degree-≤d functions both keep every coordinate's average influence below δ(d), the two families cannot be totally incompatible — some f in the support of **F**, some g in the support of **G**, and some input x must have f(x)·g(x) ≠ 0. Informally: if both sides must spread their influence thinly across many coordinates, they cannot conspire to cancel each other everywhere. The conjecture comes from Austrin–Chung–Chung–Fu–Lin–Mahmoody's work on quantum random oracles, and is load-bearing in Chung–Lin–Mahmoody's black-box separation for quantum commitments. The exact statement, its quantifier order, and the reading conventions are pinned in `CONTRACT.md`.

## Where things stand

**Rung R1 is proved, verified and frozen.** Over ℤ₂, for families of normalized cylinder-pattern indicators with windows of at most d coordinates, incompatibility is impossible once every coordinate's average influence drops below δ(d) = 1/(3d) — and the constant is tight, since the known grid construction sits at exactly 1/(2d). The proof replaces a lossy union bound with an isoperimetric "payment" argument, and proves its isoperimetric ingredient from scratch rather than citing it. It passed five independent blind referee passes across two model families, a triage that upheld nothing, and a final context-free blind gate. Artifact: `proofs/0023-prover-1.md`.

**Rung R2 is open, and partly proved.** R2 widens the class from bounded windows to *all* degree-≤d subsets of the cube. Three things are now known. At d = 2 the rung is true with the optimal constant: the exact frontier is 1/4, established by proof rather than search, so widening the class does not move the frontier at all. At every threshold below the already-known exponential one, the rung holds by a new and independent route. And at d = 3 the frontier 1/6 survived an exhaustive probe that completely enumerated the window-6 and window-7 pattern classes — 16.7 million and 126 million patterns, cross-checked by an exact combinatorial identity rather than by rerunning the same code. What remains open is precisely the inverse-polynomial window in between, which is the conjecture's actual content.

## Cycle 3 in narrative

The rung's declared prerequisite was a literature errand, and it returned more than a citation. The number of coordinates a degree-≤d Boolean function can depend on is bracketed in print between 3·2^(d-1)−2 and 4.394·2^d. That matters because the obvious way to lift R1's proof — substitute this junta size for R1's window size — is therefore capped at exponentially small thresholds by a *lower* bound, not merely by a weak upper bound. No future sharpening of constants rescues it. The same sweep found a barrier-flavoured fact: every coordinate such a function depends on carries influence at least 2^(−1−d), so exponentially many coordinates at exponentially small influence each total Θ(d) — the whole "pay per coordinate used" idea can never yield more than the free budget that every unit-norm degree-d function already has.

It also found the campaign's most useful import: a shattering theorem (Chang–Fang) saying a nonzero degree-D function's support projects onto all coordinates outside some set of size at most D, for *arbitrary field-valued* functions. An independent verification pass read all fourteen pages and confirmed the attribution character for character, corrected one non-mathematical detail, and — more valuable — found the paper's group-uniform version, whose degree notion is printed identically to the Contract's. So the mechanism reaches the top rung over every finite abelian group, not just ℤ₂.

Two obstructions were then certified by executable search rather than argued: payments denominated in relevance windows die at exponentially small thresholds, and any route needing a single coordinate to be influential for *both* functions fails outright. A strategist pass reconciled these against the shattering mechanism, ruling that they do not reach it — the shattering window is bounded by a theorem, not by counting relevant coordinates — and it checked this by evaluating both killer families on their own shattering windows.

Then the lead attempt refuted its own key step. The reduction and a new positivity lemma went through unconditionally, giving the exponential-threshold result and a genuinely new two-family consequence of the shattering theorem. But the payment hypothesis is false: an explicit pair of complementary sets on d+1 coordinates pays d·2^(1−d), so the constant fails from d = 3 onward, with an exact-arithmetic certificate to d = 12. The strategist's calibration had tested only d ≤ 2, where the value happens to be exactly 1 — a two-point fit that looked compelling and was a coincidence of small cases. The repaired form relocates the difficulty to a single-function influence question, which is recognisably Aaronson–Ambainis-flavoured.

A separate attempt turned the two obstructions into a stated internal barrier, proving two caps and ruling that the lead routes escape — while correcting the strategist's *reason* for that escape. This artifact then went through the full five-pass loop, and the loop earned its cost. The three passes on one model family cleared it or found only minor issues; the two on the other family found substantive problems. Triage ruled 10 findings upheld, 9 overruled, 6 pedantic — and **overturned both "clean" verdicts**, each of which had affirmatively cleared four real defects. The most consequential correction: the barrier's scope sentences quantified over the whole technique class when only two sub-classes are capped, so **ladder rung R4 is not condemned** after all. That claim would otherwise have quietly removed a named rung from the approved plan. Triage also overruled the sharpest objection — that the escape analysis had evaluated the wrong window — with evidence from the plan's own definitions. A revision applied all ten fixes without new mathematics; two focused re-verification passes are in flight.

Finally, a cheap milestone designed to kill the lead constant came back negative in the most useful way: it *proved* the relevance payment floor is exactly 1, attained precisely when the two sides share a single relevant coordinate — a class that includes the published extremal pair at every d. Coverage included every cross-disjoint pair at N ≤ 4 (42.9 million, integer-exact), every pair with at most four relevant coordinates per side at *every* N, and the entire degree-≤2 class, plus checks over four non-Boolean groups. The floor reproduces the published ceiling exactly, and a larger constant would contradict it — independent self-consistency of the accounting.

## What this does not establish

R2 is not proved. Neither is ℤ₂-PCC (which needs the statement for all degree-d functions, not just set indicators), and neither is PCC. Nothing from cycle 3 is ESTABLISHED in the campaign's sense: the two caps are conditional pending re-verification, and both cycle-3 proof artifacts are honest PARTIALs. Four rungs beyond R2 remain, with the ladder's own difficulty assessment marking a change in kind — from combinatorial to genuinely analytic, sign-and-cancellation arguments — at R4.

## Lessons worth carrying

- **Small-case calibration misleads.** A constant fitted at d ≤ 2 predicted the frontier exactly and was wrong from d = 3. Any constant should be tested at the first d where the structure is not degenerate.
- **Affirmative clearances are not evidence.** Twice now a referee's "no defect here" has been false, once on an enumerable claim. Triage is not optional overhead.
- **Model-family diversity did the work.** On the barrier artifact, one family's three passes missed what the other family's two passes found. Five samples of one model would have accepted an artifact that condemned a live rung.
- **Refuters that fail usefully.** Three of five refuter runs found no counterexample and were valuable anyway: two produced proofs (a frontier value, a payment floor), and all three produced obstruction diagnoses that redirected the proving effort.
- **Citation hygiene has a blindness cost.** Four of five referees had to consult files outside their permitted view, all self-disclosed, purely because dependency rows cited source cards by internal block id instead of filename. Fixed in the revision.
