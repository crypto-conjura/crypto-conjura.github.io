## How the paper leaves it open

`paper-asks-question`. Settled for perfect completeness (Theorem 3.1: $d_{\mathsf{A}} d_{\mathsf{B}} / \lambda$ classical queries, success $1 - \lambda$), and explicitly left open for imperfect completeness. For two quantum parties with imperfect completeness the paper proves a barrier (Theorem 7.6): a classical polynomial-query attack there would imply the Simulation Conjecture. That barrier's protocol requires both parties to be quantum, so it does not apply to the classical-Alice case stated here, and no barrier is known for it.

### Unsupported by the paper

- "The proof has three ingredients: a quantum heavy-query learner generalising [BMG09]" (setting_latex), said of Theorem 3.1. The CAQB attack (Construction 3.7, p. 17) uses classical heavy-query learning for Alice; Lemma 3.6 is titled "Bounding the classical heavy queries" and Lemma 3.8's proof calls the efficiency argument "identical to" that of [BM17]. Page 5 attributes the quantum heavy-query learner to the proof of Theorem 1.3, the QAQB result.
- "and no barrier is known for it" (status_note). The paper never asks whether any barrier applies to the classical-Alice imperfect-completeness case, so it supports neither the presence nor the absence of one.
- "both parties run the hard-to-simulate quantum algorithm and compare their estimates" (setting_latex). In Protocol 7.7 (p. 30) the parties exchange only a uniform shift r; no estimate is ever sent or compared.
- The claim that the eavesdropper's query bound is worst-case (definitions_latex, Definition 2). Both Lemma 3.8 (p. 17) and the proof of Theorem 3.1 (p. 19) bound only the expected number of queries.
- "No bound is imposed on ... the output length m(κ)" is presented as the paper's position (parameters, m). Theorem 3.1's bound d_A·d_B/λ is indeed m-free, but p. 6 describes the same result as yielding "a poly(d, m) ≤ poly(κ)-query attack", so the paper is not uniformly on the draft's side here.

### Corrections the checker asked for

- **setting_latex** — Claims the proof of Theorem 3.1 uses "a quantum heavy-query learner generalising [BMG09]". Construction 3.7 (p. 17) uses a purely classical heavy-query learner for Alice; Lemma 3.6 is titled "Bounding the classical heavy queries" and Lemma 3.8's proof says the efficiency argument is "identical to" that of [BM17]. The quantum heavy-query learner is attributed on p. 5 to the proof of Theorem 1.3 (the QAQB result), not Theorem 3.1.
  - suggested: The proof has three ingredients: the classical heavy-query learner for Alice of [BM17, BKSY11], which needs no quantum generalisation here because Alice is classical; an independence lemma (Lemma 3.2) saying that, conditioned on the transcript and the oracle, the joint state of the two parties is a product state; and a consistency lemma (Lemma 3.3) saying that if the eavesdropper's learned partial oracle misses the maximal Fourier monomial of the joint state, some full oracle is consistent with both her sampled fake Alice view and the real execution.
- **definitions_latex (Definition 2) and formal_statement_latex** — Requires the eavesdropper's query complexity to be bounded "in the worst case over t and its coins". Theorem 3.1's bound is on the expected number of queries: Lemma 3.8 (p. 17) and the proof of Theorem 3.1 (p. 19) both say "expected", and Construction 3.7 has no worst-case bound.
  - suggested: Definition 2: "Its query complexity is the expected number of classical queries it makes, over the choice of $h$, the execution, and its own coins." Formal statement: "$\mathsf{E}$ makes at most $q(\kappa)$ classical queries to $h$ in expectation" (or, if a worst-case bound is wanted, state explicitly that it is obtained from the expected bound by truncation at the cost of an additive constant in $\lambda$).
- **setting_latex** — States the Simulation Conjecture in its polynomial-QUERY form and then says its falsity yields the Theorem 7.6 protocol. Theorem 7.6 (p. 30) is conditioned on Conjecture 7.2, the quantum polynomial-TIME simulation conjecture; the polynomial-query version is Conjecture 7.1 (p. 29), which Lemma 7.8 handles only in its "Furthermore" clause, where the honest parties become polynomial-query rather than polynomial-time.
  - suggested: "...if the folklore Simulation Conjecture [AA14] is false -- in the polynomial-time form the paper calls Conjecture 7.2, that the acceptance probability of any polynomial-time quantum oracle algorithm can be estimated to within $1/\mathrm{poly}$ by a polynomial-query classical algorithm -- then..."
- **setting_latex and status_note** — Drops the quantifier restricting Theorem 7.6's guarantee to an infinite set of security parameters. Theorem 7.6 (p. 30): "there exists a single-bit key agreement protocol with respect to an infinite set $\mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ such that ... For all $\kappa \in \mathcal{U}$, the protocol has $(1-2^{-\kappa})$-completeness and $2^{-\kappa}$-IND-security".
  - suggested: "...then there is a single-bit QCCC protocol and an infinite set $\mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ such that for every $\kappa \in \mathcal{U}$ the protocol has $(1-2^{-\kappa})$-completeness and $2^{-\kappa}$-IND-security against classical $\mathrm{poly}(\kappa)$-query eavesdroppers."
- **setting_latex** — Says that in Protocol 7.7 both parties "compare their estimates". They never exchange or compare estimates: p. 30, steps 3--4, Alice sends only a uniform shift $r \in [0,1]$ and each party independently rounds $2^{\ell}p(\cdot) + r$.
  - suggested: "Crucially, in that construction \emph{both} parties run the hard-to-simulate quantum algorithm and independently round their own empirical estimates against a common random shift that Alice sends; a classical Alice cannot do her half."
- **status_note** — Asserts "no barrier is known for it". The paper nowhere discusses whether the Section 7 barrier extends to a classical Alice; that the barrier's own construction does not (Protocol 7.7, p. 30) is an inference the draft is entitled to draw, but the absence of any other barrier is not something the paper supports.
  - suggested: "That barrier's protocol (Protocol 7.7) has both parties run the hard-to-simulate quantum algorithm, so it yields no classical-Alice counterexample; the paper does not address whether some other barrier applies to the classical-Alice case."

## What to check hardest

(1) The paper poses this as a question (``an intriguing question for future work''), not as a conjecture, and does not itself commit to the positive direction; the direction stated here is the natural extension of its own Theorem 1.1, and a reviewer should be comfortable with that framing before publishing. (2) Scope: the paper's sentence covers ``all of our attacks in this work'', which includes the conditional quantum-Alice attack; I have narrowed to the classical-Alice case because that is what ``the above result'' in the same sentence refers to, and because the paper's own Section 7 barrier rules out an unconditional statement for two quantum parties. Anyone editing this must not widen it back to quantum Alice, which would silently make it imply the Simulation Conjecture. (3) The claim that the Section 7 barrier does not apply to a classical Alice is my reading of Protocol 7.7 and Theorem 1.4, both of which have both parties running the quantum algorithm $Q$; the paper does not say this in so many words. (4) I have fixed the parameters asymptotically (polynomial queries, negligible completeness error, constant success), matching the regime of Section 7, rather than asking for the concrete $d_{\mathsf{A}} d_{\mathsf{B}} / \lambda$ rate; whether the quadratic rate survives is a further question the paper does not pose. (5) I have not checked whether this has been resolved since February 2022, nor whether the classical attack of \cite{BM17} in the fully classical model already handles imperfect completeness -- this paper only cites the perfectly complete presentation of \cite{BKSY11} -- and either would change how the problem should be framed.
