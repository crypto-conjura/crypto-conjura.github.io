## C. Techniques on this exact family, and what obstructs each

C1. *Union bound / one-window-covers (conjunction subclass).* For
conjunction patterns, RelInf_i = 1/2 for every window coordinate (card S1,
App B conventions), so average influence = (1/2)·window-membership
marginal; then E|J_f ∩ J_g| ≤ 2δ·d < 1 at δ < 1/(2d) gives a
disjoint-window pair, which is automatically compatible. Obstruction to
generalising: for general patterns the identity FAILS — a pattern can
mention a window coordinate while giving it exponentially small influence
(use it on a 2^{-Ω(d)} fraction of the window cube). [Scout's own
analysis, not a literature claim.]

C2. *Spread lemma (ALWZ) and successors* [RESTATED — Lovett's PCMI lecture
notes; second-moment proof arXiv:2209.11347]. Answer to the task's
specific question: **spreadness does NOT match low average influence.**
ALWZ q-spreadness demands Pr[S ⊆ W] ≤ q^{|S|} for ALL sets S (all levels);
R1's hypothesis is a level-1 bound only, and in the wrong measure —
influence rather than window membership. Low window-marginals imply low
average influence (Inf_i ≤ 1 per function), but NOT conversely, by C1's
obstruction. Under the *stronger* hypothesis "window marginals ≤ δ", R1 is
trivial at δ < 1/(2d) by C1's computation with no spread lemma needed. So
spread machinery attacks a hypothesis R1 does not grant; the rung's entire
content is the gap between influence and window membership — coordinates
patterns use weakly. [Scout's own analysis.]

C3. *ACC22 block domination* [READ — card S1 §5.2 mechanism]: already
gives the full class at 2^{-d}/d; the |𝒴|^d loss is structural
(Cauchy–Schwarz across all nonconstant blocks at once).

C4. *Separator tree + OSSS* (B3 + OSSS [RESTATED — Eskenazis lecture
notes, standard statement]): the only poly(d) import; per-pair, promise
and distributional lifts open. This is the campaign's plan P2, not prior
art.
