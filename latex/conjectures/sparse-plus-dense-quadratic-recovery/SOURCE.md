## How the paper leaves it open

`paper-conjectures`. Open as a theorem. The paper proves exact polynomial-time recovery when the polynomials are drawn i.i.d. from a nice distribution (Theorem 2), which does not include this blockwise-random distribution, and reports experiments in which the modified semidefinite program recovers the planted input for $m > 3 \cdot (\text{number of variables})$ at $n + n' = 60$ and $120$. No proof for this family is given anywhere in the paper.

### Unsupported by the paper

- The input distribution is presented as a second obstruction that puts the candidate outside the paper's Definition 1. Definition 1 (PDF p. 5) constrains only the polynomial distribution Q and never mentions the input, and the paper's remark "On the distribution of x" (PDF p. 6) explicitly allows any x with E x = 0 that is O(E||x||^2/n)-sub-Gaussian, which the half-and-half mixture chi_{B_1,B_2}^n is. The paper attributes the difficulty solely to the polynomials being a sum of a random dense and a random sparse polynomial.
- "the mixture creates difficulty in the analysis" is presented as the paper naming the input mixture as the obstruction. The paper's words are "this combination creates theoretical difficulty in the analysis" (PDF p. 6), where "this combination" is the sum of a random dense and a random sparse polynomial.
- "correlation to the planted solution reaching 1 by m/n approx 2" (progress_note). Figure 2 (PDF p. 18) shows about 0.95 at m/n = 2.00, with 1 attained only near m/n = 3.
- "the companion conjecture \cite{BBKK} that no expanding weak quadratic generator exists at all". That is this paper's own Hypothesis 1 (PDF p. 4), not a result or conjecture of [4].
- "It is also the last gap between the paper's theorems and its stated position that it knows of no degree-two candidate that survives its attacks." The paper makes no such claim; its own Hypothesis 1 (PDF p. 4) is unproved, and it states outright that it has no attack on the degree-3 candidates (PDF pp. 4, 19-20).
- "a question of interest to the matrix-recovery literature independently of the cryptographic motivation" is asserted as though the paper says so. The paper notes only that matrix RIP is not known to apply to sparse ensembles (PDF pp. 8, 12); it claims no independent interest for the mixed-sparsity question.

The draft also glosses the paper's phrase "this combination" (dense plus sparse polynomials) as covering the input mixture.
  - suggested: Only one feature takes this candidate outside Definition 1 of the paper, and it concerns the polynomials alone: the coefficient variances are wildly non-uniform across the two blocks, since on the first block only n/2 of the binom(n+1,2) quadratic monomials are present while on the second every coefficient is, so no single normalisation makes all variances equal to one (compare the paper's footnote 3, PDF p. 5). The obstruction the paper names is exactly that each polynomial is the sum of a random dense and a random sparse polynomial: "this combination creates theoretical difficulty in the analysis".

## What to check hardest

Three things to check. (1) The paper's Section 4.2 writes the sparse-part coefficients as $\alpha_i, \beta_i, \gamma$ with no index $j$, so read literally the same coefficients are shared across all $m$ polynomials and only the matching $\sigma_j$ varies. I have taken them to be fresh per polynomial, which is what the paper's own Julia code in Appendix A does (\texttt{genmatrixsmq} redraws every coefficient for each of the $m$ samples). A reviewer should confirm this reading against the original Lin-Matt paper, since the shared-coefficient reading gives a genuinely different and much more structured problem. (2) The parameters $B_1, B'$ and $C$ are "set arbitrarily" in the paper, and $B$ --- described elsewhere as the magnitude of the allowed perturbations --- enters only through $B_2 \ge nB^2 + nBB_1$, so the constraint effectively just forces $B_2$ to dominate $nB_1$. I have quantified over all such settings bounded by a fixed polynomial in $n$; the polynomial boundedness is my explicit addition, though it is forced by the paper's framing (the planted solution must consist of polynomially bounded integers for the enumeration step to be efficient). (3) The paper says only "it is possible to recover in this case as well", without naming a success probability or a sample threshold. I have stated exact recovery with probability $1 - o(1)$, by analogy with Theorem 2 (which achieves $1 - n^{-\log n}$ from $m \ge n(\log n)^{O(1)}$), and I have kept the candidate's own stretch $m \ge n^{1+\varepsilon}$ rather than the theorem's $n(\log n)^{O(1)}$; a reviewer may reasonably prefer the weaker target of distinguishing, or approximate recovery, which is what the experiments actually measure (correlation with the planted solution). I have not checked whether the Lin-Matt candidate was subsequently withdrawn or amended, which would not close this question but would change how much anyone cares.
