## How the paper leaves it open

`paper-states-open`. Settled affirmatively whenever the classical implication from $\mathcal{Q}$ to one-way functions is witnessed by a black-box reduction between the two security games, with the implementation reduction arbitrary, for both uniform and non-uniform quantum adversaries and also for reductions that assume a deterministic adversary. Open when that security implication is not witnessed by such a black-box reduction; the paper takes no position on which way it goes.

### Corrections the checker asked for

- **definitions_latex (Definition 4, "game-based black-box" clause)** — The clause omits that the reduction is given 1^{1/eps} as an input parameter, and states the oracle adversary's success as "non-negligible advantage" instead of the paper's "advantage eps(n_1) for all security parameters n_1" (Def 5, p.10, with footnote 8 explaining the input is needed for non-vacuity, and p.18 stating that knowing eps is crucial to the proof of Theorem 2, Case 2). The looser clause makes the black-box case, and hence the region the status_note reports as settled, wider than the paper proves.
  - suggested: ... the existence of A_Q in clause 2 is established by a black-box reduction: a PPT oracle algorithm R that is additionally given 1^{1/eps} as an input parameter, such that for every (possibly inefficient) adversary A winning in C_{P,g_f} with advantage eps(n_1) >= 1/poly(n_1) at every security parameter n_1, the algorithm R^A wins in C_{Q,f} with advantage delta_eps >= 1/poly(n_2) above t_Q, where R may query A on inputs of its choice, on several security parameters, and may rewind A or reset its randomness.
- **formal_statement_latex and definitions_latex (Definition 4, closing remark)** — "equivalently, the classical existence of Q implies the classical existence of one-way functions" and "clause 2 is a bare existential statement, and is exactly the assertion that the classical existence of Q implies the classical existence of P" state an equivalence where only one direction holds. Definition 8 (p.11) is a per-implementation condition quantified over every PPT f in F_Q; it yields existence-implies-existence, but existence-implies-existence does not yield a map f -> g_f with the security property. The paper claims only that the definition "formalizes what it means for a primitive's existence to imply that of another" (p.11).
  - suggested: In the formal statement: "...exists in the sense of Definition 4; this is the paper's formalization of the classical existence of Q implying the classical existence of one-way functions, and in particular it entails that implication." In Definition 4: "Neither clause carries any black-box requirement; clause 2 is a bare existential statement, and clauses 1 and 2 together entail that the classical existence of Q implies the classical existence of P."
- **informal** — "the reduction may inspect the code of the attacker, and a quantum attacker has no classical code to inspect" is contradicted by the paper's own Definition 2 (p.8), where an efficient quantum algorithm is a circuit family whose descriptions are computed by a classical PPT algorithm; and the paper offers no diagnosis of why the non-black-box case is open.
  - suggested: Delete the claim, or restrict it: "...the reduction may inspect the code of the attacker, and while a uniform quantum attacker is still generated by classical code, its state -- and, in the non-uniform case, its quantum advice -- has no classical description. The paper does not say why this case resists; it simply records it as unresolved."

## What to check hardest

The main thing a reviewer should press on is whether this is a problem or a programme. The paper poses it as a question but gives no account of what makes the non-black-box case hard, and offers no partial result towards it. "Non-black-box security reduction" is not a structured class the way black-box reductions are; the hypothesis of the conjecture, as the paper's Definition 8 makes explicit, is just the bare implication "$\mathcal{Q}$ classically exists $\Rightarrow$ one-way functions classically exist", so a positive proof would have to be a general lifting theorem with no reduction structure to grip. The honest reading may be that the affirmative direction is not attackable, and only the refuting direction (a contrived primitive, in the style of the paper's own $4$-message counterexample) has a visible line of attack. A second point: the paper leaves it open without conjecturing a direction, so phrasing it affirmatively is a site convention, not the authors' claim, and a solver should treat both directions as live. Third, I have not checked the literature after December 2024 for a resolution, and the paper is short and recent enough that a follow-up could exist. Finally, the third "Furthermore" clause of Corollary 1 (deterministic-adversary reductions) is asserted for one-way functions on the strength of the falsifiability of the one-way function game; I have kept the conjecture free of any deterministic-adversary hypothesis, matching the black-box statement the paper proves.
