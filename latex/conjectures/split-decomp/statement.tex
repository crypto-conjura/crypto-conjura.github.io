\cjkicker{CONJURA OPEN PROBLEM}
\cjtitle{Parallel Presampling}
\cjsubtitle{From split auxiliary input to split bit-fixing}
\cjstatus{Statement: AI-written, not yet formalized. Open at every
  $\ell \ge 2$. The case of deterministic preprocessing is immediate
  from~\cite{CDGS} (Remark~\ref{rem:det}), so the whole content lies in
  the private coins. This document states the conjecture and nothing
  else; what follows from it, with proofs, is~\cite{PP}.}
\cjcategory{\emph{Idealized Models \& Non-Uniformity}.}

\vspace{0.4em}

\begin{informalconjecture}
Several parties read an entire random function, without communicating and
on independent coins, and each writes down a short string. An online
adversary is then given all the strings and a bounded number of queries to
the function. The conjecture says that each party can, on its own, mark a
short list of points of the function, so that the online adversary cannot
tell the real function from one fixed in advance on all the lists together
and fresh everywhere else. Because the parties mark points of the
\emph{same} function, no two lists disagree where they overlap, so no
arbitration is needed.
\end{informalconjecture}

\section{The setting}\label{sec:setting}

Presampling is the standard route to non-uniform security in idealized
models. A random oracle together with $S$ bits of oracle-dependent advice
is close to a mixture of oracles fixed on $P$ points and uniform
elsewhere, at a cost of roughly $ST/P$ against $T$-query
adversaries~\cite{Unruh,DGK,CDGS}. The technique assumes a \emph{single}
preprocessing stage, and the reduction carrying a bit-fixing bound back to
the auxiliary-input model~\cite[Thm.~5]{CDGS} exploits that assumption
directly: the bit-fixing attacker simulates the one preprocessor
internally, on a table it samples itself, and can therefore inspect the
whole of that table together with the whole of the advice before deciding
which points to fix.

The conjecture below asks for the analogue with $\ell \ge 2$ preprocessing
stages running in parallel: they read the same oracle, use independent
private coins, and never communicate. The obstruction is not that any one
leakage is long. It is that no bit-fixing preprocessor can see the other
stages' leakage, because that leakage depends on private coins it does not
hold, and the points needing to be fixed are in general a function of all
of them.

Two things follow from it, and are proved from it in~\cite{PP}. Any bound
already proved in the \emph{ordinary} bit-fixing model transfers to $\ell$
parallel preprocessing stages, so that existing bounds need not be
reproved; and, running that transfer on one application, a random oracle
extracts randomness from $\ell$ independent unpredictable sources ---
which is the application~\cite[\S1.4]{CFHS} raises the decomposition
question for, compression being what stalls on the non-monolithic
constructions a decomposition route is wanted for.

\section{Notation and parameters}\label{sec:notation}

For $n \in \mathbb{N}$ write $[n] := \{1,\dots,n\}$. All logarithms are
base two, and $x \sample \mathcal{X}$ is a uniform draw from a finite set,
independent of all others unless stated otherwise. Algorithms are
computationally unbounded and may be randomized, so that ``algorithm'' may
be read as ``arbitrary, possibly randomized, function''; only query counts
are ever bounded. An \emph{absolute constant} does not depend on any
parameter below. Indices $i$ and $j$ range over $[\ell]$.

Fix throughout:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\ell \in \mathbb{N}$ with $\ell \ge 2$, the number of parallel
  preprocessing stages.
\item $N, M \in \mathbb{N}$, and $\Fun := \{\, f : [N] \to [M] \,\}$, a
  function being identified with its table.
\item $S_1,\dots,S_\ell \in \mathbb{N}$, the advice lengths of the stages.
\item $P_1,\dots,P_\ell \in \mathbb{N}$, the numbers of points the stages
  may fix.
\item $\gamma \in (0,1)$, an additive slack.
\item $\mathcal{R}_1,\dots,\mathcal{R}_\ell$, the private coin spaces.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[split preprocessing]\label{def:pre}
A \emph{split preprocessing} is a tuple of randomized maps
$f_i : \Fun \times \mathcal{R}_i \to \bits^{S_i}$, one per stage, written
$z_i = f_i(F;r_i)$. Each stage reads the whole of $F$ and draws its own
$r_i \sample \mathcal{R}_i$; the coins $r_1,\dots,r_\ell$ are
\emph{mutually independent} and no stage sees another's coins or output.
Write $\vz := (z_1,\dots,z_\ell)$ and $\vr := (r_1,\dots,r_\ell)$.

This is the preprocessing of a split attacker against the \emph{split
auxiliary-input} oracle $\SAI$, which samples $F \sample \Fun$, hands it
at the preprocessing interface separately to each of the $\ell$ parties,
and answers the online interface by $F$ itself. The conjecture's purpose
is to replace that oracle by the \emph{split bit-fixing} oracle $\SBF$,
which instead accepts from party $i$ a list of at most $P_i$ query/answer
pairs and answers online from the merged lists where they are defined and
from a fresh table elsewhere. Neither model is needed to state the
conjecture; both are set out in~\cite[\S3]{PP}.
\end{definition}

\section{The conjecture}\label{sec:conj}

The object asked for is a tuple of \emph{selectors}, one per stage, each
reading the whole oracle but only its own leakage and its own coins.

\begin{definition}[split selector tuples]\label{def:selector}
A \emph{split selector tuple} with budgets $(P_1,\dots,P_\ell)$ is a tuple
of maps
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \Lambda_i &\;:\; \Fun \times \bits^{S_i} \times \mathcal{R}_i
  \;\longrightarrow\; 2^{[N]} \times [M]^{*}, \\
  \Lambda_i(F,z_i,r_i) &\;=\; (I_i, a_i),
\end{aligned}
\]
for $i \in [\ell]$, with $|I_i| \le P_i$ and $a_i : I_i \to [M]$. The
tuple is \emph{consistent} if $a_i = F|_{I_i}$ for every $i$ and all
inputs, that is, if each selector fixes its points to the values the
oracle actually takes there.

Given $F$, $\vz$ and $\vr$, write $I := \bigcup_i I_i$ and let
$Y[\Lambda]$ denote the random function taking the value
$(a_1 \merge \cdots \merge a_\ell)[u]$ at each $u \in I$, where $\merge$
is the priority merge --- at $u$ it takes $a_i(u)$ for the least $i$ with
$u \in I_i$ --- and an independent uniform value of $[M]$ at each
$u \notin I$. Once $(F,\vz,\vr)$ is fixed, $Y[\Lambda]$ is a
$P$-bit-fixing source for $P := \sum_i P_i$, and $|I| \le P$ always.

Under consistency the merge rule is immaterial, since for
$u \in I_i \cap I_j$ both selectors fix $u$ to $F(u)$; it is written into
the definition only so that $Y[\Lambda]$ is defined for the free variants
of the conjecture discussed in~\cite[\S6]{PP}.
\end{definition}

\begin{conjecture}[parallel decomposition]\label{conj:main}
There is an absolute constant $c$ such that the following holds for all
$\ell \ge 2$, all $N$ and $M$, all split preprocessings
$f_1,\dots,f_\ell$ with output lengths $S_1,\dots,S_\ell$, all
$P_1,\dots,P_\ell \in \mathbb{N}$ and all $\gamma \in (0,1)$. There is a
\emph{consistent} split selector tuple $(\Lambda_1,\dots,\Lambda_\ell)$
with budgets $(P_1,\dots,P_\ell)$, depending only on
$f_1,\dots,f_\ell$, $P_1,\dots,P_\ell$ and $\gamma$, such that, writing
\[
  \Delta(T) \;:=\;
  c\,T\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{S_i + \log\gamma^{-1}}{P_i} ,
\]
the following two bounds hold for every $T \in \mathbb{N}$ and every
distinguisher $D$ that takes a tuple of strings and makes at most $T$
oracle queries. Draw $X \sample \Fun$, then $r_1,\dots,r_\ell$
independently, set $z_i := f_i(X;r_i)$, and draw $Y[\Lambda]$ as in
Definition~\ref{def:selector} \emph{without} resampling $X$, $\vz$ or
$\vr$. Then
\[
  \Bigl|\, \Prob{D^{X}(\vz) = 1} - \Prob{D^{Y[\Lambda]}(\vz) = 1}\,\Bigr|
  \;\le\; \Delta(T) + \gamma ,
\]
and, with $\Delta^{+}(T)$ denoting $\Delta(T)$ with each
$\log\gamma^{-1}$ replaced by $2\log\gamma^{-1}$,
\[
  \Prob{D^{X}(\vz) = 1}
  \;\le\; 2^{\Delta^{+}(T)}\cdot\Prob{D^{Y[\Lambda]}(\vz) = 1} + 2\gamma .
\]
\end{conjecture}

\section{What the quantification is doing}\label{sec:remarks}

Five points are the statement rather than decoration. Each is developed,
with proofs where there are any, in~\cite{PP}.

\begin{remark}[reduction to the known case]\label{rem:reduces}
Taking $f_j$ constant, so $S_j = 0$, and $P_j = 1$, for every $j \ge 2$,
the statement is Lemma~1 of~\cite{CDGS} up to the constant $c$, with the
single difference that $f_1$ is permitted to be randomized. That
difference is free: the entropy bookkeeping the single-source proof rests
on survives randomized leakage verbatim~\cite[Lem.~1]{PP}. The entire
content of the conjecture is that the fixed set may be presented as a
union $I_1 \cup \cdots \cup I_\ell$ whose parts are chosen without
reference to one another's leakage.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the selectors are split, and that is the difficulty]
\label{rem:separate}
Running the single-source selector on each $z_i$ separately and taking the
union fails already at $\ell = 2$. Identify $[M]$ with $\mathbb{Z}_M$, let
$f_1$ draw $u \sample [N]$ and output $\bigl(u,\sum_{x\ne u}X(x)\bigr)$,
let $f_2$ draw $v \sample [N]$ and output
$\bigl(v,\sum_{x\ne v}X(x)\bigr)$, and take $f_j$ constant for $j \ge 3$.
Each leakage constrains $X$ by one linear equation on $N-1$ coordinates,
so each conditional source is dense and the single-source selector fixes
nothing; but given both, and $u \ne v$, the equations differ by
$X(v)-X(u)$, and the two-query $D$ that reads the oracle at $u$ and at
$v$ --- both named by its input --- distinguishes with advantage $1-1/M$.
Setting $I_1 := \{u\}$ and $I_2 := \{v\}$, each computable from its own
leakage alone, repairs it. Whether that is a general phenomenon is exactly
what is open. The example is~\cite[\S1.4]{CFHS}'s attack.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[why the selectors read the whole oracle]\label{rem:allofF}
$\Lambda_i$ is given $F$ in full, and not merely $z_i$. That is what makes
consistency achievable, and consistency is what makes the lists compatible
where they overlap, so that no arbitration rule is needed and the
statement is symmetric in the stages. It is also what the single-source
construction already does. The selectors are \emph{not} given the other
stages' leakage or coins, and that restriction, not the input $F$, is
where the difficulty lies.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[quantifier order]\label{rem:order}
The selector tuple is chosen after $P_1,\dots,P_\ell$ and $\gamma$ but
\emph{before} $T$ and before $D$. Relaxing this makes the statement easier
in a way neither consequence in~\cite{PP} can use: both fix their query
budget only after the attacker, and the extraction theorem there needs one
tuple to serve two distinguishers at two different budgets.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the shape of the error term is extrapolated]\label{rem:shape}
\cite{CDGS} fixes $\Delta(T)$ only at $\ell = 1$. That the stages
contribute a \emph{sum} $\sum_i (S_i + \log\gamma^{-1})/P_i$, one term per
stage of the single-source shape, is what one gets by charging each stage
for its own leakage against its own budget and charging nothing for the
interaction between them. Nothing establishes it beyond $\ell = 1$. A true
statement with an extra factor $\phi(\ell)$, or with the budgets entering
through $\min_i P_i$, would leave both consequences structurally intact
and change every bound they produce. The constant $c$ is the one thing
this statement is casual about; the functional form is a second.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[unpredictable advice is not assumed, and marks the hard
case]\label{rem:det}
No hypothesis is placed on how guessable the $z_i$ are, deliberately. At
one extreme, if each $z_i$ is a deterministic function of the table the
transfer is immediate from~\cite{CDGS} and needs no conjecture: one stage,
which reads $F$, recomputes every other $z_j = f_j(F)$ itself, applies the
single-source selector of~\cite[Lem.~1]{CDGS} to $z_1\|\cdots\|z_\ell$
with budget $P$, and presets it, the others presetting nothing.
Unpredictable advice therefore \emph{strengthens} the challenge rather
than easing it: it is exactly the regime in which no stage can reconstruct
another's advice. Applications may supply such a hypothesis for their own
reasons; the extraction theorem of~\cite{PP} does, and its hypothesis is
about the \emph{points} the advice encodes, not about the advice itself.
\end{remark}
