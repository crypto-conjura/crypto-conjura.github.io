### Unsupported by the paper

- Status note: "Theorem 5 ... specializes to γ < 1/4 at ε = 0" — presented as content of Theorem 5, whose stated range is ε ∈ (0,1/3) (p22). The specialization does follow from the definitions, but the paper does not state it.
- Subtitle "arbitrary protocols" and informal "necessarily by protocols that do something useful with messages whose signers they do not recognize" — the paper says only that "new techniques would be needed for this that go beyond multi-verifier signatures of work" (p22), pointing at a stronger setup primitive rather than at non-simple protocols over the same setup.
- Execution model: "The adversary is PPT in λ" with unrestricted corruption timing — p16 specifies static corruption of up to t parties by an Interactive Word RAM machine bounded to c steps per round, and p17 adds a per-round message bound θ.
- Notation: "ε ∈ [0,1)" — every place the paper quantifies ε it uses an open interval, ε ∈ (0,1) in Lemma 1 (p18) and ε ∈ (0,1/3) in Lemma 3 and Theorem 5 (pp20, 22).

### Corrections the checker asked for

- **formal_statement_latex / informal / subtitle** — The statement freezes the setup as a (γ,0)-partial PKI and declares that any resolution must be a non-"simple" protocol over that setup. The paper's own proposed route (p22) is transferable proofs of work → a *ranked* partial PKI → Dolev-Strong, which leaves the (γ,ε)-partial PKI abstraction entirely, so Theorem 6 does not bear on it. The draft therefore poses a strictly stronger problem than the paper and rules out the paper's preferred answer.
  - suggested: State the conjecture as the paper does: for every constant γ < 1/2 there is a consensus protocol achieving resilience γ in the paper's permissionless CRS setting — by any means, including a setup abstraction stronger than a (γ,ε)-partial PKI (e.g. the ranked partial PKI the paper points to). Drop "arbitrary protocols" from the subtitle and drop the "necessarily by protocols that do something useful with messages whose signers they do not recognize" clause from the informal. If the (γ,0)-fixed-setup version is kept, label it explicitly as a strengthening chosen by the harvester, and keep the Theorem 6 rider only under that labelling.
- **definitions_latex (Execution model)** — The adversary is described as "PPT in λ" with no restriction on when corruptions happen and no per-round budget. Page 16 states that both parties and adversary are Interactive Word RAM machines with O(log λ)-bit words, that each party including corrupted ones has a concrete bound of c computational steps per round, and that the adversary "can corrupt up to t parties (statically)"; page 17 adds an upper bound θ on the number of messages the adversary can send per round and the slowdown parameter β.
  - suggested: "The adversary is active/Byzantine and statically corrupts up to t parties. Parties and adversary are Interactive Word RAM machines with O(\log\lambda)-bit words; every party, honest or corrupted, executes at most c computational steps per round, the adversary sends at most θ messages per round, and a moderately hard function output requiring r steps for an honest party requires at least β·r adversarial steps." At minimum, "statically" must be added, since the conjecture as written quantifies over adaptive adversaries the paper's model excludes.
- **notation_latex (ε range) and status_note** — The notation declares ε ∈ [0,1) and the status note presents "specializes to γ < 1/4 at ε = 0" as content of Theorem 5. Theorem 5 and Lemma 3 (p20, p22) both require ε ∈ (0,1/3), an open interval that excludes 0, and Lemma 1 (p18) takes ε ∈ (0,1).
  - suggested: Record Theorem 5's range as ε ∈ (0,1/3) and γ < (1/4)(1−3ε). Present the ε = 0 case as a consequence rather than as the theorem: since a (γ,0)-partial PKI satisfies the (γ,ε) conditions for every ε > 0, choosing ε small enough that γ < (1/4)(1−3ε) yields consensus for every γ < 1/4 at ε = 0. Also note that ε = 0 is not a setting PartialPKISetup can realize (Theorem 4 with Lemma 1 gives ε > 0), so the conjecture at ε = 0 is narrower than the paper's question about its own setting.

## What to check hardest

Four things to check. (1) The paper's sentence about reaching $1/2$ says ``in our setting''; I have read that as the partial PKI setting, because that is where both the achieved threshold and the impossibility are stated, but it could also be read as the full permissionless CRS model, in which case the effective resilience is $\beta\gamma$ and the slowdown factor $\beta$ would have to enter the statement. (2) I fixed $\epsilon = 0$ to line up with Theorem 6, which is stated at $\epsilon = 0$; the paper's own protocol runs at $\epsilon \in (0,1/3)$ with a threshold degrading as $\frac{1}{4}(1-3\epsilon)$, so a solver may prefer the $\epsilon$-parametrized version, and a proof at $\epsilon = 0$ does not automatically give one at $\epsilon > 0$. (3) The proof of Theorem 6 in Appendix B.3 says ``for simplicity we consider a deterministic protocol'', so even the impossibility for randomized simple protocols is arguably not fully covered — a reviewer should not overstate what is closed. (4) The paper's remark that transferable proofs would ``come closer'' to a ranked partial PKI suggests the route to $1/2$ may already be known to distributed-computing folklore given transferability; the open content is achieving it without transferable proofs, or building transferable ones.
