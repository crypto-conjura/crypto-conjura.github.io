## How the paper leaves it open

`paper-conjectures`. Entirely open. The paper proves that two relaxations of it (distributional black-box helpfulness and class helpfulness) follow from an unproved amplification conjecture about Simon's oracle, but neither the relaxations nor that conjecture are established, and the complementary statement that one-way functions are black-box useless for collision resistance is also open.

### Unsupported by the paper

- The concrete CRHF syntax and parameters in definitions_latex (key k in {0,1}^lambda, input x in {0,1}^{2lambda}, output in {0,1}^lambda): the paper contains no CRHF definition anywhere - Section 2.2 (PDF pp.10-11) defines only OWFs, key agreement, and (a)IO.
- "the first natural inhabitant of the gray zone between separation and uselessness" and "would give the first natural example": PDF p.29 says only "a natural (and important) example", and §6.4 (PDF p.33) already presents the backdoored-random-oracle case as "a relatively natural example" of the same phenomenon, so "first" is both unsupported and in tension with the paper.
- "The compiling-out separations and the transcript-sampling separations treated elsewhere in the paper do lift to uselessness": PDF p.29 says these techniques "can be fully or partially adapted"; the transcript-sampling results lift only under restrictions (unbalanced/perfectly-correct/constant-round/one-round key agreement) and general key agreement is left open (PDF pp.7, 13).
- "Because a single conjecture about Simon's oracle cannot be pushed through for arbitrary primitives, the paper also formulates two relaxations of the conjecture": an invented causal explanation; the paper's stated reason (PDF p.29) is that Conjecture 6.1 is hard to prove and the relaxations probe its plausibility, and the technical obstruction it actually records (PDF p.32) is that the measure-one set of auxiliary implementations depends on the one-way function.
- "The primitive F of one-way functions has as implementations all functions F": not stated in the paper; Def. 2.6 (PDF p.10) defines a one-way function as an oracle-aided PPT machine relative to an oracle, and F_OWF is only ever used, never defined.

### Corrections the checker asked for

- **definitions_latex (Definition: Collision-resistant hash functions)** — Presented among the paper's genuine definitions, but the paper defines no CRHF at all - Section 2.2 (PDF pp.10-11) covers only OWFs (Def. 2.6), key agreement (Def. 2.7), and (a)IO (Def. 2.8). The concrete parameters (key in {0,1}^lambda, input in {0,1}^{2lambda}, output in {0,1}^lambda) are the drafter's, and they fix a compression ratio the paper leaves open.
  - suggested: State CRH generically - the primitive of keyed compressing hash functions, an adversary breaking H if it finds a collision under a random key with non-negligible probability for infinitely many lambda - and add an explicit note that the paper leaves the syntax and compression parameters unspecified, so any standard instantiation is intended. If concrete parameters are kept for readability, label them as the drafter's instantiation, not the paper's.
- **definitions_latex (Definition: One-way functions)** — "has as implementations all functions F" is not stated in the paper; Def. 2.6 (PDF p.10) defines an OWF as an oracle-aided PPT machine relative to an oracle O, with security against PPT adversaries and negligible advantage.
  - suggested: Keep the break relation (it is the correct negation of Def. 2.6's negligibility requirement, and Def. 2.2 does quantify over arbitrary functions A), but note that F_OWF as a set of implementations is a reconstruction from the paper's usage "F in F_OWF" (p.31) rather than a definition the paper gives.
- **setting_latex / informal ("the first natural inhabitant", "the first natural example")** — The word "first" is not in the paper, and it contradicts §6.4 (PDF p.33), where the backdoored-random-oracle example is itself offered as "a relatively natural example of an idealized primitive which does not black-box imply CRHFs, yet is BB helpful for CRHFs."
  - suggested: "a natural (and important) example of a primitive which does not black-box imply another primitive, yet is black-box helpful for it" (PDF p.29), dropping "first".
- **setting_latex ("The compiling-out separations and the transcript-sampling separations treated elsewhere in the paper do lift to uselessness")** — Overstates the paper. PDF p.29: the techniques "can be fully or partially adapted to the black-box uselessness setting." The transcript-sampling lifting is restricted - Thm 4.1 needs perfect correctness plus an unbalanced construction, Thm 4.9 needs constant-round imperfect KA with constant OWF queries, Thm 4.12 covers one-round Merkle-type protocols - and general KA is explicitly left open (pp.7, 13).
  - suggested: "The compiling-out separations lift to uselessness in full (Thms 5.6, 5.7); the transcript-sampling separations lift only partially, giving uselessness of OWFs for perfectly correct unbalanced, constant-round imperfect, and one-round key agreement, with the general case left as an open conjecture."
- **setting_latex ("Because a single conjecture about Simon's oracle cannot be pushed through for arbitrary primitives, the paper also formulates two relaxations of the conjecture")** — Invented rationale. PDF p.29 gives the reason as "While we believe that Conjecture 6.1 holds, proving it seems quite challenging. Towards getting a better understanding of its plausibility, we introduce two natural relaxations." The specific obstruction the paper does record is different: on p.32, "the measure 1 of implementations Z in F_Z needs not be the same for every F, which is the reason why we cannot extract from this proof a universal primitive."
  - suggested: "Because proving Conjecture 6.1 seems quite challenging, the paper formulates two relaxations to gauge its plausibility - one using distributional black-box reductions, one using class reductions in the sense of Shaltiel - and shows both follow from the amplification conjecture. The distributional route falls short of the full conjecture because the measure-one set of auxiliary implementations depends on the one-way function, so no universal auxiliary primitive can be extracted; restricting to efficient one-way functions is what recovers a single implementation via Borel-Cantelli."

## What to check hardest

(1) The paper does not give a formal definition of collision-resistant hash functions in its preliminaries (Section 2.2 defines one-way functions, key agreement and obfuscation only), so the $\mathcal{CRH}$ definition above is a standard keyed formalization supplied by me; a reviewer should check that nothing in the conjecture turns on keyed versus unkeyed, or on the compression ratio. (2) The paper says of Section 6 that it maintains "a relatively informal discussion", so the primitive-level unfolding of "fully black-box helpful" above, though it matches the paper's own informal definition and its formal distributional analogue (Definition 6.4), is a reconstruction. (3) Refuting the conjecture means proving that one-way functions are fully black-box useless for collision resistance, which is a strong statement; a would-be refuter should be clear which direction they are attacking. (4) I am not aware of a resolution since January 2021, but I have not exhaustively surveyed follow-up work on one-way product functions or on Simon's oracle.
