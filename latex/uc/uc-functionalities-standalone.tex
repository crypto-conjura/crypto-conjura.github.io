\section{Scope and ordering principle}

This document indexes 100 ideal functionalities of the universal composability
framework \cite{c01,c20}. Entries are ordered by \emph{dependency depth}
rather than by importance or by chronology: Layer 0 collects what must be
assumed, and each subsequent layer is realizable, at least in principle, from
functionalities appearing earlier.

Two conventions require comment.

First, a primitive earns an entry when it exposes a party-facing interface
whose ideal behavior can be stated independently of any implementation. Local
hardness-based primitives such as one-way functions and pseudorandom
generators fail this test: there is no cross-party interface to idealize, and
the composition theorem yields nothing that a hybrid argument does not already
give. They appear below only so that their absence is recorded explicitly. The
partial exception is \Func{crypto} \cite{kt11,bpw03}, which is worth having
precisely because keys are reused across sessions, that is, only once joint
state is present.

Second, several entries in the framework literature are not functionalities
but \emph{modifiers} applied to existing ones. These are collected separately
in Section~\ref{sec:modifiers}, because the cross product of modifiers and
functionalities generates coverage far more economically than a flat list.

\subsection*{Status markers}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,style=nextline,itemsep=0.15em]
  \item[\StS] Idealized setup. Not realizable in the plain model; assumed
    rather than constructed.
  \item[\StC] Canonical. An established functionality with a widely cited
    reference definition.
  \item[\StE] Emerging. A UC treatment exists but the formulation is not yet
    standardized, or competing formulations coexist.
  \item[\StO] Open. No established UC formulation. Writing one is a research
    contribution, not a citation.
\end{description}

\noindent
Entries marked \StO{} carry no reference. Where a reference is marked with a
dagger ($\dagger$), the bibliography entry needs independent verification
before use; see the \texttt{note} fields in the companion \texttt{.bib} file.

\section{Notes on coverage}

\paragraph{Where the open entries cluster.}
The \StO{} markers fall almost entirely in Layer 2 and in the functional
encryption band of Layer 3. This is structural rather than accidental. Layer 2
consists of local computations with no inter-party interface. The functional
encryption band concerns what a key holder learns from a ciphertext, and
simulation-based security there is impossible in general for unbounded key
queries \cite{bsw11}. Entry 42 sits on the boundary between the two: the
security statement for a memory-hard function is a cost lower bound rather
than an indistinguishability, so it resists UC formulation for a third reason
again.

\paragraph{Realizability boundaries worth recording alongside each entry.}
\Func{COM}, \Func{ZK}, \Func{OT} and most of \Func{SFE} are unrealizable in
the plain model \cite{cf01,ckl06}. \Func{BC} as usually written is not
realizable against adaptive adversaries, and must be weakened
\cite{hz10,gkkz11}. \Func{coin} with guaranteed output is unrealizable for two
parties, so the abort variant is the operative one. \Func{smt} under adaptive
corruption requires non-committing encryption \cite{cfgn96}, which is the same
commitment problem that blocks naive symmetric-encryption functionalities.

\paragraph{Coverage sweep, 2022 to 2026.}
Recent work was located by web search and by querying the IACR ePrint archive;
Google Scholar blocks automated queries, so no claim of exhaustiveness is made
here, and the sweep is biased toward papers whose titles announce composability.
It changed the table in five places rather than merely adding rows. Threshold
FHE decryption \cite{zzlp25} moves \Func{FHE} from open to canonical, retiring one
of the longest-standing gaps. Password-hardened encryption \cite{abgg25} enters
as a new category adjacent to \Func{MHF}: it is the one
recent functionality that touches password hashing, and the same paper reports a
flaw in the proof of an earlier threshold construction. Adaptor signatures
\cite{grs25} enter as \Func{adsig}. Ring signatures \cite{yo07,bdw22} and
self-tallying elections \cite{ecclesia25} acquire anchors they previously
lacked. Most consequentially, the global restricted observable generic group of
\cite{bfkt24} now anchors \GFunc{GG}, which had no reference at all: it stands to
the plain generic group exactly as \GFunc{RO} stands to \Func{RO}, and it
establishes UC security of a deployed proof system without asymptotic overhead.
The complementary result that some deployed zkSNARKs are unconditionally
UC-secure in the random oracle model \cite{cf24} strengthens \Func{SNARK}.

Two merges preserved the count. The bilinear group models fold into \GFunc{GG},
since a bilinear group is a generic group with a pairing and the observable
global functionality generalizes both. Verifiable delay functions fold into
\Func{TLP}. Both follow the practice already used for
\Func{PRF} with \Func{PRP} and for \Func{COT} with \Func{ROT}. The merges are
lossy in one respect worth recording: a verifiable delay function is publicly
verifiable and a time-lock puzzle need not be, so the row understates a real
distinction.

\paragraph{Deliberate exclusions.}
Witness encryption, aggregate and multi-signatures, fair exchange, electronic
cash, cross-chain bridges, and composable analyses of operating-system
services \cite{cplus11} were cut to hold the count at 100. Each is a
reasonable candidate; none has a settled functionality that would displace an
entry above.

\paragraph{Two source bibliographies.}
Entries are anchored against two published reference lists: that of the UC
framework paper \cite{c01,c20}, and that of the IITM model paper
\cite{kt13,k06}. The second contributes one new functionality, ideal
Diffie-Hellman exponentiation \cite{kr17}, which is the realizable counterpart
to assuming a generic-group setup and therefore now anchors \GFunc{GG} as well as
\Func{DH}. It also supplies the joint-state theorems for encryption and
signatures with local computation \cite{kt08} and their
session-identifier-free refinement \cite{kt11ccs}, both of which enter the
modifier index rather than the main table. Where the two lists overlap they
agree on page and volume data for seven entries, which are marked
\texttt{status:cross-checked} in the companion \texttt{.bib} file. They diverge
once: the UC list cites Canetti and Herzog's J. Cryptology 24(1) article, the
IITM list a distinct TCC 2006 paper \cite{ch06}. Both are retained.

Two entries were displaced to hold the count at 100. Quantum and classical
composability \cite{bm04} is a framework variant rather than a functionality and
moves to the paragraph above. Witness encryption remains excluded for the reason
given earlier.

\paragraph{Defects in the source reference list.}
Four issues in the attached UC bibliography affect anyone mining it for
citation keys. The entry keyed \texttt{[b01]} has an empty author surname; the
author is Barak \cite{barak01}. The key \texttt{[y82]} is used twice, for
\emph{Theory and Applications of Trapdoor Functions} and for \emph{Protocols
for Secure Computation}, both FOCS 1982; they are split here into
\texttt{y82a} and \texttt{y82b}. The entry \texttt{[bpw07]} appears twice
verbatim across a page break. Finally, \texttt{[cdpw07]} is titled
\emph{Universally Composable Security with Pre-Existing Setup}, whereas the
published TCC 2007 title reads \emph{with Global Setup}; a title variant
rather than an error, but it will not match a DBLP lookup.

\paragraph{Framework variants and further limitations.}
Retained in the bibliography without a table entry, since they bear on how any
of the above is stated rather than on what is stated: joint-state composition
\cite{cr03,kt08} and its session-identifier-free refinement
\cite{kt11ccs,blr04}, global subroutines \cite{bchtz20}, the simplified variant
for standard MPC \cite{ccl15}, the IITM model \cite{k06,kt13}, GNUC \cite{hs11}
and SUC \cite{w16}, RSIM \cite{bpw07}, task-PIOAs \cite{cckllps06}, abstract and
constructive cryptography \cite{mr11,maurer11}, local adversaries \cite{cv12},
quantum and classical composability \cite{bm04}, the runtime notions
\cite{hmqu05}, the comparison of simulatability notions \cite{kdmr08}, the
realizability obstruction for game-derived functionalities \cite{ddmrs06}, the
incompleteness of oblivious transfer in a synchronous model \cite{hm04a}, the
trusted-setup-free relaxations \cite{ps04,bs05}, and symbolic analysis
\cite{ch06}. Trapdoor functions \cite{y82a} are listed to resolve the key
collision noted above.
