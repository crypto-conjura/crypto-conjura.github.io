\section*{Reader's guide}

\noindent\textbf{The idea, in one sentence.} The conjecture asks for \emph{some}
family of $P$-mixtures; it never asks that the fixed sets be large, and
Definition~\ref{def:bf-here} bounds them by $|I| \le P$ rather than fixing $|I|$. So when the
requested $P$ exceeds what the proof can afford, do not use it --- build the family
at the balance point instead. A $P_0$-mixture with $P_0 \le P$ \emph{is} a
$P$-mixture.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{What that buys.} The hypothesis $(\mathrm{H1})$ of the campaign's
Theorem~E$''$, which caps $P$ at roughly $\sqrt{\sigma'q^{+}/\delta}$, disappears.
The cost is the constant $C$, which moves from $8$ to $13$; the constant $c$ does
not move.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{What it does not buy.} Two things, and both were overclaimed in
the first draft of this work before referees caught them.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item The family must be allowed to depend on $q$. The Contract's
  Remark~\texttt{rem:order} forbids that: the family is chosen after $P$ and
  $\gamma$ but \emph{before} $q$. So Theorem~H proves a relaxation, and the window
  the campaign records as open --- absolute $C$, $q$-free family --- stays open.
\item Keeping the family $q$-free costs a factor $\sqrt{q^{+}}$ in the constant
  (Theorem~H$'$). It is tempting to conclude that the $\sqrt{q^{+}}$ therefore
  \emph{is} the price of $q$-independence. That does not follow, and it is not
  claimed here: what is shown is that $q$-dependence \emph{suffices} to remove the
  factor within this route. No lower bound against $q$-free families exists
  anywhere in the campaign.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{What remains.} The hypothesis $(\mathrm{H2})$, a condition on the
output size $M$, is carried unchanged through everything below. It is the whole
residue on the $P$-axis, and \S\ref{sec:h2} is about what it means and whether
applications satisfy it. They do for key extraction and do not for long-output
extraction.

\section{Setting}

Fix $N, M \ge 2$ and let $\Fun := \{f : [N] \times [N] \to [M]\}$, with
$H \gets_{\$} \Fun$. A pair $(S_1, S_2)$ of sources is \emph{split} when the two
use independent coins and neither sees the other's output; each reads all of $H$
and outputs a point $x_i \in [N]$ together with leakage $z_i$ with
$|z_i| \le \sigma_i$. The pair is \emph{$\delta$-unpredictable} when no predictor
given $H$ and the whole leakage vector guesses either point with probability more
than $\delta$. All algorithms are computationally unbounded; only queries count.

Throughout, with logarithms to base $2$,
\[
  \sigma := \sigma_1 + \sigma_2, \qquad
  \sigma' := \sigma + 2\log N, \qquad
  q^{+} := q + 1, \qquad
  S := \sqrt{\sigma' q^{+} \delta},
\]
\[
  t_q := \sqrt{\sigma' q^{+}/\delta}, \qquad
  t_0 := \sqrt{\sigma'/\delta}, \qquad
  t_q = t_0\sqrt{q^{+}}, \qquad
  B := \sigma' + \log \gamma^{-1}.
\]
Note $\delta \ge 1/N$ always, since a uniform guess predicts $x_i$ with probability
$1/N$; and $\sigma' \ge 2$, which needs $N \ge 2$ and is used three times below.

\begin{definition}[bit-fixing sources, and consistency]\label{def:bf-here}
A distribution $Y$ over $\Fun$ is \emph{$P$-bit-fixing} if there are a set
$I \subseteq [N] \times [N]$ with $|I| \le P$ and a map $a : I \to [M]$ such that a
draw $g \gets Y$ satisfies $g(u) = a(u)$ for every $u \in I$ and is uniform on
$[M]$ and independent across the points $u \notin I$. A \emph{$P$-mixture} is a
finite indexed family of $P$-bit-fixing components; it is \emph{consistent with}
$f$ when $a(u) = f(u)$ on $I$ for every component.
\end{definition}

The inequality $|I| \le P$ is the whole hinge, and it is worth pausing on. It is
not a stylistic choice: the same convention appears verbatim in
Coretti--Dodis--Guo--Steinberger, whose Definition~1 reads ``$P$-bit-fixing if it
is fixed on \emph{at most} $P$ coordinates''.

\section{The construction}

Given a requested $P \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\gamma \in (0,1)$, put
\[
  \gamma_0 := \max(\gamma,\, N^{-2}),
  \qquad
  P_0 := \min\bigl(P,\ \lceil t_q \rceil\bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathcal{Y} := \mathcal{Y}^{P_0, \gamma_0/2},
\]
where $\mathcal{Y}^{\cdot,\cdot}$ is the family the campaign's Theorem~E$''$ takes
as given. Two observations license this.

\begin{lemHz}[admissibility]
Every member of $\mathcal{Y}$ is a $P$-mixture consistent with its index;
$\mathcal{Y}$ is indexed by $(f,\zeta)$ and by nothing else; and Theorem~E$''$
applies to it at $(P_0, \gamma_0)$.
\end{lemHz}

\begin{proof}
Each member is a $P_0$-mixture, so each component has fixed set $I$ with
$|I| \le P_0 \le P$. Definition~\ref{def:bf-here} asks $|I| \le P$ and nothing
more, so the same witness $(I,a)$ that proves $P_0$-bit-fixing proves
$P$-bit-fixing. The uniformity and independence conditions off $I$ refer to the
same unchanged $I$, and consistency is a property of the values $a$, so both
survive verbatim. The index set is unchanged, as \texttt{rem:index} requires. For
Theorem~E$''$: $\gamma_0 \in (0,1)$ since $\gamma < 1$ and $N^{-2} \le 1/4$;
hypothesis $(\mathrm{H1})$ reads $P_0 \le t_q + 1$ and holds because
$P_0 \le \lceil t_q \rceil \le t_q + 1$; and $(\mathrm{H2})$ does not mention $P$,
so it is unaffected.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemHo}[three slack bounds]
$\log \gamma_0^{-1} \le \log \gamma^{-1}$; $\ \log \gamma_0^{-1} \le \sigma'$, hence
$\sigma' + \log \gamma_0^{-1} \le 2\sigma'$; and $N^{-2} \le \tfrac{1}{4}S$.
\end{lemHo}

\begin{proof}
$\gamma_0 \ge \gamma$ gives the first, and it is what keeps $c$ at $2$: without it
the clamp would inflate the leading term rather than the additive one.
$\gamma_0 \ge N^{-2}$ gives $\log \gamma_0^{-1} \le 2\log N \le \sigma'$. For the
third, $\delta \ge 1/N$, $\sigma' \ge 2$ and $q^{+} \ge 1$ give
$S \ge \sqrt{2/N}$, so
$N^{-2} \le \tfrac14 S \iff N^{-3} \le \tfrac12$, true for every $N \ge 2$.
\end{proof}

\section{The two theorems}

\begin{thmH}[at fixed $q$, no hypothesis on $P$]
Let $N,M \ge 2$, fix $q \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$ and suppose $(\mathrm{H2})$. For
every $P \in \mathbb{N}$ and every $\gamma \in (0,1)$, the family $\mathcal{Y}$
above consists of $P$-mixtures consistent with their indices, depends only on
$(S_1, S_2, P, \gamma, q)$, and satisfies, for every $q$-query
challenge-oblivious observer $D$ and with no restriction on challenge resolution,
\[
  \Adv_{\mathcal{Y}, D}
  \ \le\ \frac{2(\sigma' + \log \gamma^{-1})\,q^{+}}{P}
  \ +\ 13\,S \ +\ \gamma .
\]
\end{thmH}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~H\textlf{0} licenses Theorem~E$''$ at $(P_0, \gamma_0)$, giving
$\Adv \le 2(\sigma' + \log \gamma_0^{-1})q^{+}/P_0 + 8S + \gamma_0$. Two cases.

\emph{Case (a), $P \le \lceil t_q \rceil$.} Then $P_0 = P$, and
$\log \gamma_0^{-1} \le \log \gamma^{-1}$ makes the first term at most
$2Bq^{+}/P$, the target's own. With $\gamma_0 \le \gamma + \tfrac14 S$ the total is
$2Bq^{+}/P + 8\tfrac14 S + \gamma$.

\emph{Case (b), $P > \lceil t_q \rceil$.} Then $P_0 = \lceil t_q \rceil \ge t_q$,
and $\sigma' + \log \gamma_0^{-1} \le 2\sigma'$ gives
\[
  \frac{2(\sigma' + \log \gamma_0^{-1})q^{+}}{P_0}
  \ \le\ \frac{4\sigma' q^{+}}{t_q}
  \ =\ 4\sigma' q^{+}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\sigma' q^{+}}}
  \ =\ 4S,
\]
so the total is at most $12\tfrac14 S + \gamma$, and the target's first term is
non-negative.

Both cases are dominated by $2Bq^{+}/P + 13S + \gamma$.
\end{proof}

\noindent This is the Contract's conjectural \emph{display} at $c = 2$, $C = 13$,
with no hypothesis on $P$. It is not the conjecture: that requires the family to
depend only on $(S_1, S_2, P, \gamma)$, and a $q$-dependent family satisfies a
relaxation of it. Of the five extra units in $C$, four are case~(b)'s capping and a
quarter is the $\gamma$-clamp; three quarters go unused.

\begin{thmHp}[one $q$-free family, every $P$, at a cost of $\sqrt{q^{+}}$]
Let $N,M \ge 2$ and suppose $(\mathrm{H2})$ holds at \emph{every}
$q \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$ --- equivalently $M \le \sigma'/(2\delta)$, the
intersection binding at $q = 1$. Then the family
$\mathcal{Y}' := \mathcal{Y}^{P_0', \gamma_0/2}$ with
$P_0' := \min(P, \lceil t_0 \rceil)$ depends only on $(S_1, S_2, P, \gamma)$ ---
not on $q$ --- and satisfies, for every $P \in \mathbb{N}$, every
$q \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$ and every $q$-query challenge-oblivious observer $D$,
\[
  \Adv_{\mathcal{Y}', D}
  \ \le\ \frac{2(\sigma' + \log \gamma^{-1})\,q^{+}}{P}
  \ +\ \bigl(4\sqrt{q^{+}} + 9\bigr) S \ +\ \gamma .
\]
\end{thmHp}

\begin{proof}
As in Lemma~H\textlf{0}, with $(\mathrm{H1})$ at $P_0'$ holding for every $q$ at once, since
$P_0' \le \lceil t_0 \rceil \le t_0 + 1 \le t_q + 1$ by $t_0 \le t_q$. The
hypothesis $(\mathrm{H2})$ is \emph{not} uniform in $q$ and is supplied at every
$q$ by assumption. Case (a) is as before. Case (b), $P > \lceil t_0 \rceil$: now
$P_0' \ge t_0$, so
$4\sigma' q^{+}/t_0 = 4q^{+}\sqrt{\sigma'\delta} = 4\sqrt{q^{+}} \cdot S$, using
$S = \sqrt{q^{+}}\sqrt{\sigma'\delta}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the hypothesis of Theorem~H$'$, and a defect worth recording]
The first draft of this theorem wrote ``suppose $(\mathrm{H2})$ holds at $q$'' and
then quantified its conclusion over \emph{every} $q$ --- leaving $q$ free in the
hypothesis and rebinding it in the conclusion. Read literally the hypothesis is
\emph{vacuously} satisfiable at $q=0$, where the relevant quantity is $0$, so the
theorem would have delivered the conjecture for every $M$ and contradicted its own
statement of what remains open. A non-vacuous witness that the readings differ:
$N = 2^{12}$, $\sigma = 0$, $\delta = 2^{-12}$, $M = 2^{16}$. There
$8S < 1$ at both $q = 0$ and $q = 1$, $(\mathrm{H2})$ holds at $q = 0$, and at
$q = 1$ it fails on both arms. Three of the six referees raised this.
\end{remark}

\section{What $(\mathrm{H2})$ is, and whether applications satisfy it}\label{sec:h2}

$(\mathrm{H2})$ asks that the residue of the campaign's extraction bound fit inside
one unit of the budget $S$:
\[
  \min\bigl\{\mu'(\min(qM, N^{2})),\ 2\delta\sqrt{M}\bigr\} \ \le\ S,
  \qquad \mu'(s) := \min\bigl(s\delta,\ 2(s\delta^{2})^{1/3},\ 1\bigr).
\]
It is not a condition about decomposition at all. It is inherited from routing the
decomposition through extraction, and it enters at exactly one step.

The second arm has a clean reading. A random function's image distribution
$\nu(v) = |f^{-1}(v)|/N^{2}$ is lumpy: throwing the pair's $\approx 1/\delta^{2}$
effective probability mass into $M$ buckets gives a balls-in-bins deviation
$\approx \sqrt{M\delta^{2}} = \delta\sqrt{M}$. The real challenge is drawn from
that lumpy law and the decomposed one is uniform off the fixed set, so the
lumpiness genuinely appears in the advantage. Exploiting it, however, requires
knowing \emph{which} values are over-represented, and that means learning $f$.
That is why $q = 0$ is free for every $M$, and why the only known lower bound
witnessing $\delta\sqrt{M}$ spends $N^{2}$ queries.

When $D$ has full challenge resolution --- as a general distinguisher
$D^{H}(y, \vz)$ does, since it may steer its queries on $y$ --- the second arm is
the smaller for $q \ge 1$, $M \ge 4$, and $(\mathrm{H2})$ becomes, in bits,
\[
  \log M \ \le\ k + \log(\sigma' q^{+}) - 2, \qquad k := \log(1/\delta).
\]
\emph{Do not ask for more output bits than one source's unpredictability}, plus a
logarithmic allowance. The $q$-dependence is only additive-logarithmic: $q$ from
$2^{0}$ to $2^{128}$ moves the allowance by $127$ bits. So the corner where
$(\mathrm{H2})$ binds is not really about query budgets at all --- it is about
asking for a long output.

The two failing rows differ in kind and only one matters. Row~4 fails
$(\mathrm{H2})$, but the bound there is $2^{-27}$ anyway, so nothing is lost that
one had. Rows~5 and~6 are the real gap: security is still $2^{-91}$ and
$(\mathrm{H2})$ blocks the statement purely for asking for a long output. That is
precisely the sponge and Merkle--Damg\aa{}rd streaming use which motivated wanting
a decomposition route in the first place.

\begin{remark}[the evidence for $(\mathrm{H2})$ does not reach the corner that binds]
The only lower bound in the campaign exhibits an observer making $N^{2}$ queries,
and $\kappa$ is monotone in $q$ --- a $q$-query observer may ignore queries --- so
a lower bound at $q = N^{2}$ constrains $\kappa(1)$ not at all. Meanwhile
$(\mathrm{H2})$ is free at $q = N^{2}$, permitting $M \le \sigma' N^{2}/(4\delta)$,
and binds at small $q$. \emph{No artifact in this campaign gives any evidence that
the extraction advantage carries $M$-dependence where $(\mathrm{H2})$ actually
bites.} The sharp question is therefore narrower than the campaign has been
recording: does $\kappa(q)$ have \emph{any} $M$-dependence for small $q$? A
refuter pass hunting a small-$q$, large-$M$ distinguisher is the cheap direction,
and the campaign has never run one.
\end{remark}

\section{What is settled, and what is not}

\noindent\textbf{Settled, on the region $(\mathrm{H2})$.} The $P$-axis, at fixed
$q$, in full: no hypothesis on $P$ survives in Theorem~H.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Not settled.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{The conjecture as stated.} Theorem~H's family depends on $q$;
  Theorem~H$'$'s constant does. Neither is the conjecture, which wants both a
  $q$-free family and an absolute $C$. The window the campaign records as open is
  exactly as open as before, only better described: the residue on the $P$-axis is
  a factor $\sqrt{q^{+}}$ in the constant under $q$-free families, on
  $M \le \sigma'/(2\delta)$.
\item \emph{The $M$-corner.} $(\mathrm{H2})$ is carried, not discharged, except at
  $q = 0$ where it is vacuous for every $M$.
\item \emph{Whether the $\sqrt{q^{+}}$ is necessary.} Only achievability is shown.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{A redirection.} The campaign's deepest effort goes into removing
the $P\delta$ term so that a \emph{large} fixed set can be afforded. At fixed $q$
that work is unnecessary: one never needs a fixed set larger than
$\lceil t_q \rceil$. That programme is aimed solely at the $q$-free statement,
where the cap must sit at $t_0$ and the $\sqrt{q^{+}}$ is real.

\section{Verification status}

\noindent This work has been through six independent blind referee passes in fresh
context, spanning two model families: five on the artifact (lenses: the
definitional step; arithmetic and constants; quantifier order; case coverage and
degenerate instances; citation fidelity) and one on three proposed simplifications.
All five artifact passes returned \textsc{defects}.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Confirmed after adversarial attempt.} Lemma~H\textlf{0} was attacked by
four referees independently, each trying to construct a reading of
Definition~\ref{def:bf-here} on which it fails; none could. The arithmetic referee
re-derived every inequality by hand, reproduced the machine check's grid sizes
exactly including collision counts, found \emph{no computational error}, and
confirmed that $c = 2$ and $C = 13$ follow from the displayed inequalities rather
than from the grid. The case analysis is exhaustive with no boundary gap. No
referee found a violation of the index-set restriction, or a leak of $D$, $\vx$ or
the challenge into either family.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Corrected.} Seven class-(A) and class-(C) findings, every one of
them a claim \emph{about} the results rather than a result: Theorem~H$'$'s
malformed hypothesis; an identification of the $\sqrt{q^{+}}$ with the Contract's
own separation that conflated a threshold in $P$ with a degradation of $C$ and
overrode a documented caution; a claim to reproduce a construction whose parameter
$A := \sigma + 2 + \log(1/\gamma)$ is not $\sigma'$; a machine check described as
reaching $q \le 10^{7}$ whose vacuity filter in fact admitted nothing above
$q^{+} = 410$, so that $\sqrt{q^{+}}$ was exercised only to $\sqrt{11}$; the
unstated standing hypothesis $N, M \ge 2$; and two summary lines scoped to a
stronger statement than the body proves.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Still owed.} The revision carrying these corrections has had
\emph{no} pass. Everything here is a corollary of the campaign's Theorem~E$''$,
which rests on a corollary sitting in an artifact with zero verification passes,
whose only referee objection was overruled and never retested. The audit verified
this work \emph{given} its dependencies; it did not touch them.
