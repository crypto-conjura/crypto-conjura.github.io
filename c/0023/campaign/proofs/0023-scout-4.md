## A. VERDICT

**NO PRIOR RESOLUTION FOUND** for R2, and the errand's headline answer:

> **$M(d)$ is exponential in $d$ as a matter of printed record.** Writing
> $M(d)$ for the largest number of coordinates a degree-$\le d$ Boolean
> function can depend on, the print gives
> $$3\cdot2^{d-1}-2\ \le\ M(d)\ \le\ 4.394\cdot 2^{d}\qquad (d\ge1),$$
> upper bound Wellens, *Discrete Analysis* 2022:19, Theorem 1.1 (1.3), p. 2
> [READ]; lower bound Chiarelli–Hatami–Saks, arXiv:1801.08564v3,
> Theorem 3.1 + §3, pp. 4–5 [READ]. So $M(d)=\Theta(2^d)$ with matching
> exponent. **I02's recorded trap is real and unavoidable**, not an artifact
> of a weak upper bound: no future improvement of either constant can lift
> the junta-substitution route above $\delta(d)=\Theta(2^{-d})$.

Three secondary findings:

1. **No hypothesis of the junta theorem fails for our objects.** The
   theorem is printed for $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$; our
   $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{ind}}_d$ members are $\{0,1\}$-valued indicators on
   $\{\pm1\}^N$ rescaled by a positive constant. Domain invariance of degree
   is **printed** (Wellens, arXiv:1903.08214v2, p. 3 [READ]); the value-range
   switch and the normalization are one-line affine remarks (flagged in card
   S6 as the scout's own, not citations). So the junta bound genuinely
   applies, and the trap cannot be dodged by the indicator restriction.
2. **The junta count is a symptom; the real barrier is the per-coordinate
   quantum.** Every coordinate a degree-$\le d$ Boolean function depends on
   carries influence $\ge2^{-1-d}$ (Fourier normalisation, un-normalized
   indicator), and this is *attained* by $\mathrm{AND}_d$ / codimension-$d$
   subcubes. So $2^{\Theta(d)}$ coordinates each costing $2^{-\Theta(d)}$:
   both exponentials are tight, and any R2 argument that pays per *used*
   coordinate is structurally capped at $2^{-\Theta(d)}$.
3. **One new, genuinely usable poly($d$) mechanism was found**, and it is
   the only thing in this sweep that clears the card bar: Chang–Fang,
   arXiv:2510.13705v3, Theorem 1.2 (p. 2) [READ] — a nonzero degree-$D$
   function's support **shatters** a set of $n-D$ coordinates. For a
   degree-$\le d$ set $A$: $A$ projects *onto* $\{\pm1\}^{[N]\setminus T}$
   for some $|T|\le d$. It is stated for arbitrary field-valued $f$, so
   unlike everything in card S6 it survives I02's generalization hypothesis
   to R3. It does **not** settle R2 (no influence, no families).

R2 itself — two cross-disjoint families of degree-$\le d$ sets under a
per-coordinate average-influence cap — appears nowhere I could find, in any
of the searched literatures.

## C. Standard techniques on this family, and what obstructs each

**C1. Junta substitution (I02's named trap).** Replace R1's window size $d$
by the junta size $M(d)$ and re-run R1's payment argument. **Obstructed,
definitively, by B1's lower bound:** the best achievable is
$\delta(d)\approx1/(2M(d))\le1/(3\cdot2^{d}-4)=\Theta(2^{-d})$. Sharpening
of I02's wording for the record: this route would strip only the $1/d$ from
K1's $2^{-d}/d$ (indeed $1/(2\cdot4.394\cdot2^d)\approx0.114\cdot2^{-d}$
beats $2^{-d}/d$ for $d\ge9$) — a constant-factor gain *inside* the
exponential regime. Dead as a proof of R2.

**C2. Pay per used coordinate.** Any argument of the form "the functions can
only use so many coordinates, and each used coordinate costs influence
$\ge q$" has $q=2^{-1-d}$ and coordinate count $\le4.394\cdot2^d$, both
tight (S6a's engine is attained by $\mathrm{AND}_d$; the count by $\Xi_d$).
**Obstruction: the two exponentials cancel to give total influence
$\Theta(d)$ — which is precisely the elementary budget I02 already has for
free.** So this whole technique class provably yields nothing beyond
$\sum_i\mathrm{Inf}_i(f)\le d$. This is a clean BARRIER-class statement the
Strategist can use as-is (§3.2 class f).

**C3. Re-base the payment on the total-influence budget $\sum_i
\mathrm{Inf}_i(f)\le d$** (I02's own suggestion). **No literature found**,
either supporting or obstructing. It remains the rung's central open
question, exactly as I02 says.

**C4. Convert degree into a degree-sized *projection* window (S7).** The
live import. $\deg\mathbf 1_A\le d\Rightarrow A$ surjects onto all patterns
off a set $T(A)$ of $\le d$ coordinates; for a cross-disjoint pair, $T=T_A
\cup T_B$ has size $\le2d$ and all the conflict must be visible inside $T$.
**Obstructions, both real and unaddressed in print:** (i) the conflicting
trace pair on $T$ may depend on the pattern chosen off $T$, whereas R1's
cylinders conflict uniformly; (ii) $T(A)$ is non-canonical and the union over
a distribution's support is unbounded, so the argument must be run pairwise
or with a covering step, and the average-influence hypothesis is a
statement about the whole family. Recorded in card S7 as a lead, unverified.

**C5. CHS/Wellens potential-function machinery** ($W(f)=\sum_i
2^{-\deg_i(f)}$, maxonomial hitting sets, $h(f)\le d^3$). **Obstructed
twice:** the potential's *unit* is $2^{-\deg_i}$, so every bound it produces
is denominated in $2^{-d}$ (this is why CHS's own conclusion is
$C\cdot2^{d}$, not $\mathrm{poly}(d)$); and the restriction identity it runs
on needs $f$ two-valued, so it dies at R3. Note the interesting near-miss:
$h(f)\le\deg(f)^3$ (CHS Lemma 1.3) *is* a poly($d$) statement — a
degree-$d$ Boolean function has a maxonomial hitting set of size $\le d^3$,
i.e. $\le d^3$ coordinates meeting every top-degree monomial. That is the
same *flavour* as C4's window and is the one poly($d$) object inside CHS;
whether it can carry an influence conclusion is not addressed anywhere I
found.

**C6. VC / Sauer–Shelah counting.** Gives density and shattering
(B3, B2) but no influence; and the counting direction (Sauer–Shelah) is
explicitly *weaker* than Chang–Fang's linear inequality (their remark, p. 2).

## E. THE DECIDING SOURCE

**Chang–Fang, arXiv:2510.13705v3, sections 3–5 (pp. 5 to end) — the
unread part of the paper carded as S7, and in particular the printed
product-space inequality (5) on p. 3, $\dim_\pi(\mathrm{supp}(f))+
\deg_\otimes(f)\ge n$, together with the "multivalued Sauer lemma / finite-
Abelian-group version" its roadmap announces (pp. 3–4).** Openly on arXiv:
read, not upload.

Why this document and not another: it is the only source found in this
sweep that (a) converts "degree $\le d$" into structure with a **poly($d$),
in fact $d$-sized, loss** rather than a $2^{\Theta(d)}$ loss, (b) is stated
for arbitrary field-valued functions, so it survives I02's generalization
hypothesis to R3, and (c) advertises a finite-product / finite-abelian-group
form, which is the *parent* Contract's setting (the Contract's degree is
exactly a character-support / Efron–Stein degree on $\mathcal Y^N$). Its
possible contents and what each implies:

* **(i) (5) is printed with $\deg_\otimes$ equal to the Contract's degree
  and $\dim_\pi$ the full-projection dimension, for arbitrary finite product
  sets $X_1\times\dots\times X_n$** (what the abstract and p. 3 lead me to
  expect). Then A stands at NO PRIOR RESOLUTION, but the campaign gains a
  **group-uniform** poly($d$) window mechanism: every nonzero degree-$\le d$
  function on $\mathcal Y^N$ realises every coordinate pattern off a set of
  $\le d$ coordinates. That upgrades C4 from a $\mathbb Z_2$ lead to a
  mechanism available at every rung and for every group — the single most
  valuable thing this sweep can produce, and it should be carded as an
  addendum to S7 before the Strategist runs.
* **(ii) Somewhere in §§3–5 there is a two-function, two-family, or
  disjoint-support statement** (nothing on pp. 1–4 or in an HTML-wide check
  suggests one, so I rate this unlikely). Then A moves to **PARTIALLY
  SETTLED** for R2 and that statement becomes the rung's lead import.
* **(iii) §§3–5 are only re-derivations (Sauer–Shelah, Sziklai–Weiner, null
  designs) with no product-space degree matching ours.** Then A stands
  unchanged, S7's Theorem 1.2 remains a $\mathbb Z_2$-only import, and C4
  must be lifted to general $\mathcal Y$ by the campaign itself (a
  Prover/Strategist task, not a literature one).

Runner-up, named only so it is on the record: Wellens DA 2022:19 §3.2
("generalized coordinate measures") — if it contains a coordinate measure
whose unit is $1/\mathrm{poly}(d)$ rather than $2^{-\deg_i}$, C5's
obstruction weakens. Low prior probability (the paper's own conclusions are
all $C\cdot2^d$-shaped), openly accessible, read-not-upload.
