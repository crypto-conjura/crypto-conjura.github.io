# REPORT — c/0023: Polynomial Compatibility Conjecture (PCC)

**Date:** 2026-08-27. **Status:** one rung of six established and frozen; the root conjecture remains open.

## Goal

The Polynomial Compatibility Conjecture, in its inverse-polynomial-influence form, is a statement about pairs of random low-degree polynomials over a finite abelian group. Fix a finite abelian group and let functions on the group's N-fold product be given their natural notions of degree (highest-order term with a nonzero coefficient) and influence (how much of a function's "energy," in the Fourier sense, depends on a given input coordinate). The conjecture says: there is some way to fix the group and a threshold function δ(d), decaying no faster than an inverse polynomial in the degree d, such that whenever two probability distributions F and G over unit-norm, degree-≤d functions both keep every coordinate's average influence below δ(d), it is *impossible* for the two families to be totally incompatible — i.e., there must be some function f in the support of F, some g in the support of G, and some input x where f(x)·g(x) is nonzero. Intuitively: if both sides are required to spread their influence thinly across many coordinates (no "over-concentration" on a few inputs), they cannot conspire to always cancel each other out everywhere. The conjecture originates in Austrin–Chung–Chung–Fu–Lin–Mahmoody's work on quantum random oracles (ACC22) and is a load-bearing ingredient in Chung–Lin–Mahmoody's later black-box separation results for quantum commitments (CLM23). The full statement, its precise quantifier order, and reading conventions are pinned in `c/0023/campaign/CONTRACT.md`; the exact conjecture text is reproduced there verbatim.

## What has been done so far

**Cycle 1 (reconnaissance).** A literature scout searched the relevant 2022–2026 record and returned the verdict NO PRIOR RESOLUTION FOUND: nobody has proved or refuted this exact statement. It did pin down three usable known results: (K1) the conjecture's analogue is already known at exponentially small thresholds, for every group, by an argument that uses less than the conjecture's full hypotheses; (K2) over the two-element group ℤ₂, an explicit extremal pair of degree-d constructions achieves per-coordinate influence 1/(2d) on both sides while still being totally incompatible — so no valid threshold can reach or exceed 1/(2d) over ℤ₂; (K3) the conjecture is consumed downstream, in exactly the inverse-polynomial regime, by the quantum-commitment separation paper. A computational refuter then searched extensively for counterexamples (candidate incompatible pairs beating an inverse-polynomial threshold) and did not find one; instead it mapped out an exact "refutation frontier" — the best influence value any known construction can reach — and found it always sits within a constant factor of 1/d, never approaching zero faster. Two of its certified data points sharpen the printed record: the exact frontier at degree 2 is 1/5 (strictly below the published 1/(2d) = 1/4 construction), and a transport argument gives incompatible pairs at influence (1−1/|𝒴|)/d for *every* finite abelian group, so the "c₂ ≥ 1" constraint, previously printed for ℤ₂ only, holds universally. It also diagnosed structural obstructions (union-bound rigidity, a union/product asymmetry, and a Boolean-value impossibility) that make constructing a counterexample look structurally hard. On this evidence, a case ladder of six rungs (R0 calibration, then R1 through R6) was designed and human-approved: a sequence of increasingly general special cases of the conjecture, climbing from small finite combinatorics up to the full statement.

**Cycle 2 (rung R1).** The campaign attacked the first genuine rung, R1: the conjecture restricted to ℤ₂ and to families of normalized "junta indicator" functions (indicators of cylinder sets depending on at most d coordinates, i.e. windows of size ≤ d). A targeted micro-scout confirmed this specific combinatorial object has no prior resolution in the literature (several plausible-looking related results — spread lemmas, cross-intersecting families, subcube partitions — were checked and each ruled out as not actually matching). A second computational search, this time exhaustive over the small cases plus large random and structured samples, found no counterexample and instead pinned the exact answer: the best achievable per-coordinate influence for an incompatible pair in this class is exactly 1/(2d), attained by the grid construction, with no richer pattern doing better. A prover then proved this as a theorem. The proof idea, in brief: recast "family F is incompatible with family G" as "every pattern in F's support has its indicator's support disjoint from every pattern in G's"; this disjointness, projected onto the shared window of ≤ d coordinates, forces a quantitative "payment" — a lower bound on the influence mass any cross-disjoint pair must spend on its shared window — proved via an edge-isoperimetry-type inequality on the hypercube that the artifact proves from scratch by induction (deliberately not cited from the literature), replacing what would otherwise be a lossy union bound. Summing this payment over coordinates and combining with the average-influence hypothesis yields the threshold directly, matching the grid construction's extremal value exactly.

**Verification.** The proof went through the full verification protocol: five independent blind reviews (three by one verifier configuration, two by a second family), each reading only the problem statement, the proof text, and the source cards, with no access to prior reasoning or verdicts. All five came back clean. A triage pass reconciled the raw findings across all five reviews — nine total findings, of which zero were upheld as real defects, three were overruled as reviewer error, and six were pedantic (gaps a competent reader fills without effort). Zero findings were tagged "needs source" or "unclear," so every objection raised against the proof was actually resolved on the mathematics, not shelved as "can't tell." Triage also caught and corrected one reviewer's false affirmative clearance — a reminder of why the adjudication layer exists. A final, fully independent blind gate review (an additional check beyond the five-pass loop, reading only the artifact and the cards) also returned clean. On this record the ledger marks the result ESTABLISHED and FROZEN, meaning it will not be re-verified or re-opened; it is now cited as a black box.

Two caveats were filed alongside the freeze, and are carried forward as real constraints, not resolved concerns: (i) the proof's technique is confirmed to be the "intended" general-purpose engine (a weighted counting/isoperimetric argument) rather than a shortcut specific to finite pattern enumeration — but this only classifies the proof as given; whether the engine actually generalizes to the next rung's broader class of functions is untested. (ii) A separate computational finding shows that at degree 2, the best incompatible pair in the *full* (non-indicator) class of functions beats the *indicator*-restricted frontier (1/5 versus 1/4) — meaning the indicator-tuned proof engine is known not to already be tight for the more general objects later rungs must handle.

## Current status

One of six planned rungs is climbed: **R1 (intermediate I01) is proved, verified, and frozen.** This establishes a genuine special case of the conjecture — restricted to ℤ₂ and to junta-indicator function families — with an explicit, tight, inverse-polynomial threshold δ(d) = 1/(3d) (constants c₁ = 1/3, c₂ = 1). **This does not establish ℤ₂-PCC** (the conjecture restricted to the two-element group, which needs the statement for *all* degree-d functions, not just indicators) **and does not establish PCC** (the full conjecture, over any group). Five harder rungs remain, and the campaign's own difficulty assessment marks a jump in kind — from combinatorial to genuinely analytic, sign-and-cancellation arguments — starting at rung R4.

## Obstructions and lessons

- The union-bound technique that suffices for R0 (plain conjunctions) and generalizes (via isoperimetry) to close R1 (junta indicators) is explicitly flagged by the campaign's own barrier pre-check as *possibly* fundamentally limited: "arguments using only per-coordinate influence budgets cannot beat exponential thresholds" is named as a candidate internal barrier, not yet established but consistent with everything observed on conjunctions. R1's proof, while succeeding, is being watched for whether it secretly still lives inside that limited technique class or genuinely breaks out of it — R2 is the first real test.
- Refuter search (exhaustive where feasible, exact-arithmetic, plus structured and randomized families) consistently fails to find any counterexample better than roughly 1/d, and the campaign's diagnosed obstructions all point the same direction: the conjecture appears true, at least over ℤ₂, and any counterexample would have to look structurally unlike anything found so far (non-Boolean, "spiky" density-valued rather than indicator-valued).
- The named kin of this problem, the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, has been open in an analogous inverse-polynomial regime since 2009 (with a since-retracted proof attempt along the way); this is the campaign's calibration for how hard the later, analytic rungs (R4–R6) are expected to be.
- A standing structural caveat: the 1/(2d) ceiling used throughout the ladder is a published fact for ℤ₂ specifically; if the ℤ₂ route is ever refuted at every inverse-polynomial threshold, that would kill ℤ₂-PCC but not the conjecture itself (the group is existentially quantified) — the ladder would then need to re-base on a different finite abelian group. The refuter's every-group (1−1/|𝒴|)/d ceiling narrows that contingency: no group escapes the c₂ ≥ 1 constraint.
